Inorganic Metallic Irritants

Copper

Acute: blue-green vomitus → metallic taste, salivation, epigastric pain, vomiting/diarrhea → hemolysis, hemoglobinuria, renal failure, jaundice → hypotension/collapse.

Chronic: green/purple gum line, metal-fume fever, neuropathy, Kayser-Fleischer ring, sunflower cataract, vineyard sprayer's lung.

PM: blue-green froth/oral mucosa → congested/hemorrhagic stomach → fatty liver + centrilobular necrosis → renal tubular necrosis + casts → hemolysis.

Tx: don't induce vomiting → ferrocyanide lavage → egg white/milk → D-penicillamine ± EDTA/BAL.

MLI: suicide relatively common; accidental food/verdigris, blue crystals in children, occupational; homicide rare; cattle poison.

Lead

Acute: lead colic + constipation, vomiting/diarrhea.

Chronic — ABCDE: Anemia + Basophilic stippling + Colic/constipation + Drop (wrist/foot) + Encephalopathy in children; also HTN, nephritis, abortions, metaphyseal bands.

PM: acute gastroenteritis → thickened/soft stomach mucosa + gray-white deposits, pallor, emaciation; lead is radiopaque.

Tx: sodium/Mg sulfate lavage → CaNa2EDTA ± BAL; diazepam for seizures; supportive.

MLI: industrial/occupational poisoning; diachylon/lead oleate associated with abortion; retained bullets can cause poisoning; chronic accidental common.

Mercury

Inorganic ingestion: corrosive GI injury — acrid taste, gray-white mouth, burning → bloody vomiting/diarrhea → oliguria + renal failure + collapse.

Vapor: pneumonitis/pulmonary edema.

Organic: ataxia + dysarthria + paresthesia + mental deterioration; chronic → Danbury/Hatter's tremor + mercurial erethism + salivation/gingivitis + acrodynia.

Methyl-Hg fish: Minamata disease.

PM: corrosive gastroenteritis, renal tubular necrosis; inhalation → pulmonary edema/pneumonitis.

Tx: oxygen + BAL / succimer (DMSA) / D-penicillamine; supportive.

MLI: occupational exposure; contaminated fish → Minamata; historically important occupational poisoning.

Arsenic

Acute: garlicky odor → burning mouth/throat → severe vomiting + rice-water diarrhea → abdominal cramps → dehydration → shock/collapse; may include QT prolongation/arrhythmias, neuropathy, renal failure, encephalopathy.

Chronic: GI upset + hyperpigmentation/“raindrop” pigmentation + hyperkeratosis of palms/soles + peripheral neuropathy + Mees' lines + alopecia → weakness/anemia. Skin lesions + neuropathy are classic.

PM: garlicky odor; inflamed/congested GI mucosa with submucosal hemorrhage; fatty liver; renal tubular degeneration; myocardial degeneration; peripheral neuritis.

Tx: remove exposure + fluids/electrolytes; BAL (dimercaprol); succimer (DMSA) / DMPS. Severe GI injury → supportive treatment; do not induce vomiting in severe corrosive GI injury.

MLI: historically important homicide because arsenic is relatively tasteless/odorless and symptoms may mimic gastroenteritis. Suicidal/accidental/occupational/environmental exposure also occurs. Chronic exposure → skin and lung cancer risk. Arsenic in hair/nails is useful for detecting chronic exposure.

METALLIC POISON SNAPSHOT
  • Arsenic → garlic + rice-water diarrhea + neuropathy
  • Lead → ABCDE + Burtonian line/basophilic stippling + wrist/foot drop
  • Mercury → tremor + erethism + acrodynia; methyl-Hg → Minamata
  • Copper → blue-green vomitus + hemolysis

Acute White Phosphorus

Mechanism: protoplasmic poison → liver + kidney + heart.

  1. Hours–days: burning GI pain, thirst, vomiting, diarrhea + garlic breath/vomit/stool.
  2. Latent stage: 2–3 days.
  3. Organ failure: acute yellow liver atrophy, renal failure, CNS collapse.
PATHOGNOMONIC
  • Luminous/smoking vomitus and stool.

Fatal dose: 60–120 mg. Fatal period: usually <24 h, may be 5–7 days.

Tx: KMnO4 lavage + activated charcoal + CuSO4 lavage + MgSO4 purgation; no oils/fats; vitamin K + fluids/shock management + dialysis if needed.

PM: garlic odor + luminous GI contents + yellow fatty liver + flabby fatty heart + enlarged greasy kidneys + dark tarry poorly coagulable blood.

Chronic Phosphorus

Phossy jaw: toothache → jaw swelling → gum necrosis → mandibular sequestration/osteomyelitis → discharging sinuses.

MLI: accidental—fireworks/rat poison; homicidal—mixed with food/drink; abortifacient; military/incendiary use.

Castor / Ricin

Ricin: toxalbumin → inhibits protein synthesis. Chewed/crushed seeds → severe hemorrhagic gastroenteritis, vomiting + bloody diarrhea → dehydration, renal failure.

PM: GI congestion, erosions and hemorrhage; organ congestion/edema. MLI: accidental in children; homicide rare; powdered seeds can cause conjunctivitis.

Abrus / Rosary Pea

Abrin: toxalbumin → inhibits protein synthesis. Crushed seeds → vomiting, bloody diarrhea/rectal bleeding → hepatic + renal + CNS damage. Injection → local necrosis + viper-bite-like picture, cardiac failure.

MLI: sui (poisoned needle), cattle poisoning/homicide; also abortifacient.

Marking Nut

Semecarpus: semecarpol + bhilawanol → powerful local irritant. Skin → itching + blistering + ulceration; mimics a bruise.

Artificial bruise clue: sharp margins, no colour evolution, no blood extravasation, blisters + itching. Used to create fabricated bruises; sometimes abortifacient.

Sea snake: myotoxic.

Venom Syndromes

  • Neurotoxic — Cobra/Krait: neuromuscular blockade → mouth → throat → respiratory muscles → paralysis → respiratory failure/coma.
  • Haemotoxic — Viper: endothelial damage + hemolysis/coagulopathy → pain, swelling, bleeding, shock, gangrene, renal tubular necrosis.
  • Myotoxic — Sea snake: muscle pain/stiffness → myoglobinuria → renal tubular necrosis.

First Aid — RIGHT

  • R Reassure; identify snake only if safe; dry bites occur.
  • I Immobilise limb with splint; pressure immobilisation for elapid/sea-snake bites.
  • G Go to hospital immediately; keep in left lateral recovery position.
  • T Tell doctor about systemic symptoms.

Clean wound with soap/water or iodine; monitor pulse, BP, SpO2, ECG; observe mild cases 24 h; give tetanus toxoid before discharge.

NEVER DO: incision, suction, cautery, cryotherapy, tissue excision, arterial-tight tourniquet.

ASV

Polyvalent: Cobra + Krait + Russell's viper + Echis. Best within 4 h; limited benefit after 8 h; doubtful after 24 h. Initial standard in the source: 10 vials (100 mL) IV over 30–60 min. Neurotoxic: repeat 10 vials after 1 h if paralysis persists. Haemotoxic: repeat after 6 h if blood remains incoagulable/bleeding continues.

ASV anaphylaxis: stop ASV → adrenaline IM 0.5 mg (1:1000) → hydrocortisone + antihistamine → repeat adrenaline 10–15 min if needed → restart ASV once stable.

Support: neuroparalysis → neostigmine + atropine; ventilate if respiratory failure; AKI → haemodialysis; blood transfusion/analgesia/antibiotics as indicated.

Medico-legal & PM

Accidental is most common; suicidal very rare (classical example Cleopatra); homicidal extremely rare; cattle poisoning occasionally by hide traders. Venom identification: colubrine → cholinesterase activity; viperine → thromboplastin activity.

External: 1–2 fang puncture marks with swelling/ecchymosis. Cobra/Krait: asphyxial signs, pulmonary edema, dark fluid blood. Viper: widespread hemorrhages, visceral congestion, renal tubular necrosis. Sea snake: muscle degeneration → myoglobinuria/renal tubular damage.

Ethyl Alcohol

Basics

Ethanol = grain alcohol; colourless, volatile, spirituous odour, burning taste; produced by fermentation of sugars by yeast.

TermKey point
Absolute alcohol99.95%
Rectified spirit90%
Proof spiritAt 10.5°C weighs 12/13 of equal volume water; proof = 2 × % alcohol by volume
Unit8 g ethanol
Arrack40–50% alcohol

Pharmacokinetics

Absorption: simple diffusion from mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine. Carbonated drinks increase absorption; food delays absorption. Peak BAC 45–90 min.

Distribution: almost all tissues; relatively little in bone/adipose tissue.

RatioValue
Blood : urine1 : 1.3
Blood : breath1 : 2100
Blood : saliva1 : 1.2

Metabolism: 90% liver, 10% unchanged via lungs, kidneys, sweat. Alcohol → acetaldehyde → acetic acid/acetyl-CoA → Krebs → CO2 + H2O. Main pathway: alcohol dehydrogenase; also MEOS and catalase-peroxidase.

MOA: ↑ GABA inhibition + NMDA inhibition → CNS depression.

Effects & BAC

  • CNS: low dose → apparent stimulation from depression of inhibitory centres; high dose → CNS depression; severe → respiratory/vasomotor centre depression → coma/death.
  • CVS: small → cutaneous/gastric vasodilation; moderate → tachycardia, mild BP changes; large → myocardial + vasomotor depression; chronic → hypertension, cardiomyopathy, AF, QT prolongation.
  • Other: ADH inhibition → diuresis; false warmth; high concentrations → erosive gastritis; chronic → megaloblastic anaemia from impaired folate metabolism.

Special Signs

  • Alcohol gaze nystagmus: jerky eye movement; ~40–100 mg%, average ~80.
  • McEwan sign: constricted pupils; slow dilatation + constriction with pain; >300 mg%.
  • Romberg: inability to stand steadily with feet together + eyes closed.
THE 4 M's
  • Micturition syncope
  • Munich beer heart → cardiac dilatation/hypertrophy in chronic heavy beer drinking
  • Morbid jealousy (Othello syndrome)
  • Mickey Finn → alcohol + chloral hydrate

Acute Poisoning

Fatal dose: 150–250 mL absolute alcohol within 1 hour. Fatal period: 12–26 h.

Tx: gastric lavage with alkaline solution; keep warm; IV fluids + 10% glucose + thiamine 100 mg + insulin; oxygen; artificial ventilation if needed; severe/deep coma → haemodialysis/peritoneal dialysis.

Chronic Alcoholism

Disulfiram: 250 mg/day → inhibits ALDH. Calcium carbimide: 50 mg/day. Supportive: chlorpromazine, clonidine, chlormethiazole; psychotherapy + Alcoholics Anonymous.

Autopsy

Acute: alcoholic odour on opening body cavities; acute gastritis + thick mucus; congestion of brain/lungs/liver; dark fluid blood; decomposition delayed.

Chronic: malnutrition; reddish-brown gastric mucosa; fatty liver → cirrhosis; dilated heart + fatty degeneration; testicular atrophy; cerebellar degeneration.

Clinical Examination of Drunkenness

Sequence: TIME → EXCLUDE → MENTAL → PHYSICAL → COORDINATION.

  • Record exact date + time.
  • Exclude mimics: hypoglycemia, stroke, epilepsy, neurological disease.
  • Mental/behavioral: appearance/dress, behavior, speech, memory, judgment, self-control, handwriting, reaction time.
  • Physical: alcoholic breath, bounding pulse, dry/furred tongue, congested eyelids, nystagmus, negative convergence.
  • Coordination: finger-nose, heel-shin, picking up small objects, lighting a match, button/unbutton clothes.

Widmark & Breathalyzer

Widmark: a = r × p × c. Distribution factor: male 0.68; female 0.55. Remember: M=68, F=55.

Henry's law / Breathalyzer: alveolar air : blood ≈ 2100–2300 : 1; 2100–2300 mL alveolar air ≈ 1 mL blood.

Major Complications

  • Delirium tremens: 72–96 h after stopping alcohol; coarse tremor, insomnia, agitation, visual/auditory hallucinations, autonomic overactivity; crawling insects/snakes are classic hallucinations; mortality 5–15%; lasts 3–4 days.
  • Wernicke encephalopathy: thiamine (B1) deficiency; GOA = Global confusion + Ophthalmoplegia + Ataxia.
  • Korsakoff psychosis: chronic B1 deficiency; AC = Amnesia + Confabulation.
  • Marchiafava–Bignami: corpus callosum degeneration.
  • Mallory–Weiss: mucosal tear at gastroesophageal junction from repeated forceful vomiting/retching → upper GI bleeding.

Methanol

Methanol = wood alcohol = carbinol; colourless, volatile liquid; odor resembles ethanol.

METHANOL = FORMIC ACID = EYES
Methanol → formaldehyde → formic acid → optic nerve toxicity.

Fatal dose: 60–200 mL. Fatal period: 24–36 h, may be delayed 2–4 days. Target organ: optic nerve. Classical complication: permanent blindness.

Clinical

  • Nausea/vomiting
  • Severe abdominal cramps
  • Headache, neck stiffness, vertigo
  • Metabolic acidosis, hypothermia
  • Convulsions, coma

Eye: blurred vision, “snow-field” vision, fixed dilated pupils, optic neuritis, optic disc hyperemia, permanent blindness.

Lab: high anion-gap metabolic acidosis; severe poisoning with blood methanol >50 mg/100 mL.

Treatment — BLOCK + BUFFER + REMOVE

  • BLOCK: ethanol or fomepizole (4-MP) → inhibit alcohol dehydrogenase.
  • BUFFER: sodium bicarbonate → correct severe metabolic acidosis.
  • FOLINIC ACID: enhances formic acid metabolism.
  • REMOVE: haemodialysis is treatment of choice in severe poisoning.

Opium & Opiates

Identity

Opium = dried juice/latex of unripe capsule of Papaver somniferum. Seeds are non-poisonous. Somniferous → CNS depressant.

ClassExamples
NaturalMorphine, Codeine
Semi-syntheticHeroin, Oxycodone, Hydromorphone
SyntheticMethadone, Pethidine, Fentanyl

Active Principles

Phenanthrene = narcotic: morphine, codeine, thebaine. Isoquinoline = non-narcotic: papaverine, noscapine.

Opioid Receptors

  • μ (Mu) — most important: analgesia, euphoria, respiratory depression, miosis, physical dependence, GI dysmotility.
  • κ (Kappa): spinal analgesia.
  • δ (Delta).
MU = Miosis + Euphoria + respiratory depression + dependence

Acute Opioid Poisoning

CLASSIC TRIAD — CPR: Coma + Pinpoint pupils + Respiratory depression.

Other findings: cyanosis, hypotension, cold skin, flaccid muscles, reduced reflexes, pulmonary edema, Cheyne–Stokes respiration. Intoxication → constricted pupils; withdrawal → dilated pupils.

Stages

  1. Excitement: euphoria, talkativeness, restlessness, hallucinations, flushing, increased mental activity.
  2. Stupor: headache, nausea/vomiting, giddiness, drowsiness, miosis, itching.
  3. Coma/Narcosis: flaccid, loss of reflexes, pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression, cyanosis, hypotension, death.

Antidote

Naloxone: competitive opioid receptor antagonist; reverses respiratory depression and CNS depression. Airway + ventilation + naloxone are core emergency principles.

Marquis Test

Conc. H2SO4 + formalin + suspected opium/morphine → purple-red → violet → blue. Marquis = Purple.

Chronic Opium Poisoning — Morphinism

Think: thin + scars + constipation + depression.

  • Emaciation, dry skin, anorexia
  • Needle marks/injection scars, abscesses
  • Constipation, furred tongue
  • Fatigue, irritability
  • Impotence/frigidity
  • Mental depression

Withdrawal

Opposite of intoxication: dilated pupils + autonomic overactivity; yawning, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, sweating, vomiting, diarrhea, increased vitals, dysphoria/restlessness. WET + WIDE pupils.

Heroin

Heroin = diacetylmorphine; extremely addictive; fatal dose ~50 mg; metabolized to 6-MAM; rapid intense euphoria → sedation. Speedball = heroin + cocaine.

Strychnine

Identity

Strychnine = neurotoxic spinal poison. Source: seeds of Strychnos nux-vomica. Extremely bitter; used as rodenticide; also called dog buttons. Active principles: strychnine, brucine, loganine.

Mechanism

STRYCHNINE = STOPS GLYCINE'S INHIBITION
Blocks glycine receptors → removes inhibitory action → uncontrolled reflex activity → violent generalized convulsions. GABA is not affected.

Clinical

Early: anxiety, nausea, mydriasis, muscle twitching → rapidly convulsions.

ROPE: R = risus sardonicus (grimacing smile); O = opisthotonus (backward arching); P = pleurothotonus (lateral bending); E = emprosthotonus (forward bending). Also trismus, generalized tonic convulsions, hyperthermia.

TWO CLASSIC POINTS: between convulsions = complete relaxation; consciousness = preserved until near death. The patient is conscious, terrified and spasming.

Fatal dose: 50–100 mg (~1–2 crushed seeds). Fatal period: 1–2 h. Main cause of death: respiratory failure.

Complications: rhabdomyolysis, myoglobinuria, acute renal failure, hyperthermia.

Treatment

  • First priority: stop convulsions.
  • Diazepam IV first-line.
  • Refractory → phenobarbital, general anaesthesia, neuromuscular blockade + ventilation if necessary.
  • Dark + quiet room; avoid noise, light and touch.
  • Decontamination: gastric lavage + activated charcoal; source notes also mention warm water + dilute KMnO4 and acidification of urine.

Postmortem

  • Postmortem caloricity — body temperature may rise after death.
  • Early onset + early disappearance of rigor mortis.
  • Asphyxial signs, GI congestion, serosal hemorrhages.

  • Send routine viscera + entire spinal cord + half brain + whole heart.

Agricultural Poisons

Big classification — I-R-H-F-N-A: Insecticides (OP, carbamates, organochlorines); Rodenticides (phosphorus, aluminium phosphide, anticoagulants, strychnine); Herbicides (paraquat, diquat); Fungicides; Nematicides; Acaricides.

Organophosphorus

OP = AChE OFF = ACh ↑
Irreversible AChE inhibition → acetylcholine accumulation → continuous stimulation → paralysis.

Routes: ingestion, inhalation, transdermal, conjunctival, injection.

Clinical picture = SLUDGE + fasciculations.

SystemFindings

CLASSIC EXAM PICTURE: miosis + salivation + sweating + bronchorrhea + fasciculations.

OP Treatment

ATROPINE + PRALIDOXIME (PAM).

Atropine: counteracts muscarinic effects. Source dose: adults 3–5 ampoules (1.8–3 mg) and children <12 years 0.05–0.1 mg/kg bolus IV every 5–20 min until atropinization.

Atropinization signs: mid-dilated pupils, HR >80 bpm, systolic BP >80 mmHg, clear air entry, dry axilla & tongue, flushing.

Once atropinized: 20% of total loading dose every hour via IV infusion in normal saline for 24–48 h, tapering gradually.

PAM / pralidoxime: reactivates phosphorylated AChE; especially useful for nicotinic effects.

ATROPINE = MUSCARINIC   |   PAM = AChE REACTIVATION + NICOTINIC

Support: ABC, oxygen/ventilation, benzodiazepines for seizures, ICU monitoring. The source also mentions morphine, aminophylline, phenothiazines and reserpine.

OP Postmortem

  • Kerosene-like smell from solvent formulation.
  • Signs of asphyxia: congested face, cyanosis of lips/fingers/nose.
  • Blood-stained froth at mouth/nose.
  • Constricted pupils.
  • Stomach contents smell of kerosene; gastric mucosa congested with submucous petechial hemorrhages.
  • Lungs: gross congestion, excessive edema, subpleural petechiae.
  • Brain, meninges and internal organs: hyperemic/congested; cerebral edema.
  • Heart microscopy: vascular dilatation, interstitial edema, inflammatory cells, hemosiderin-laden macrophages, toxic myocarditis with fatty infiltration.
  • Putrefaction delayed.

Aluminium Phosphide

Tablet pesticide → phosphine (PH3). AlP + water/HCl → PH3. Mechanism: PH3 inhibits cytochrome oxidase → cellular respiration failure → cellular hypoxia.

CLASSIC CLUE: garlic / rotten-fish odor.

Fatal dose: ~0.5 g / 1 tablet. Fatal period: 1–4 h.

SystemClinical picture
GITSevere epigastric burning, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea
CVSRefractory hypotension, cardiogenic shock, arrhythmias, myocarditis, heart failure
RespiratoryDyspnea, pulmonary edema, ARDS, cyanosis
OtherHepatic injury, AKI, altered consciousness
MOST IMPORTANT CAUSE OF DEATH
  • Cardiogenic shock.
  • There is no specific antidote — support the heart.

Treatment

No specific antidote. Supportive: IV fluids, inotropes such as dopamine/norepinephrine, activated charcoal, gastric decontamination according to appropriate toxicology protocol; MgSO4 is used in some protocols for cardiac stabilization; correct severe acidosis.

PM

Pungent garlic/rotten-fish odor on opening body cavities; stomach mucosal shedding, intense congestion and hemorrhagic spots; liver/kidneys necrotic changes and cloudy swelling; heart toxic myocarditis with fibrillar necrosis; lungs with ARDS features, congestion and pulmonary edema.

Carbon Monoxide

Properties & Sources

Colourless, odorless, tasteless, non-irritant; lighter than air; insoluble in water; burns with blue flame. Sources: incomplete combustion, fires, coal gas, automobile exhaust (1–7% CO), faulty heaters, tobacco smoke, burning fuels, endogenous production.

Mechanism

  1. Hemoglobin: CO + Hb → carboxyhemoglobin (COHb). CO affinity for Hb ≈250× O2 → anemic hypoxia.
  2. Oxygen dissociation curve: shifts left → Hb holds O2 more tightly → ↓ tissue O2 delivery.
  3. Cellular respiration: inhibits cytochrome a3 oxidase and cytochrome P450.
  4. Myoglobin: binds CO; ~15% of body CO; affinity ≈40× O2 → myocardial depression.

Characteristic Findings

  • CO bullae: erythematous blisters over pressure points (calves, buttocks, wrists, knees) due to skin hypoxia; resemble 2nd-degree burns.
  • CO automatism: erratic swinging/escaping movements disturbing furniture, clothes and surroundings before collapse.
  • Ocular: flame-shaped retinal hemorrhages; bright-red retinal veins.

Treatment

Immediately remove from exposure → fresh air → 100% O2 via tight-fitting reservoir mask or endotracheal tube. Hyperbaric oxygen at 2–3 atmospheres is listed in the source. Supportive: mannitol for cerebral edema, IV fluids, inotropes for shock/hypotension.

Postmortem

CHERRY RED
Skin, mucous membranes, blood, postmortem lividity and internal viscera. Classically associated with COHb >30%.
  • Brain: bilateral basal ganglia/globus pallidus necrosis, especially in delayed deaths.
  • Heart/lungs: subepicardial petechiae, myocardial necrosis, pulmonary edema.
  • Blood: pinkish-red and fluid.

Hydrogen Cyanide

Properties & Sources

Gas/liquid; bitter almond odor. Hydrocyanic acid = prussic acid. Sources: fruit kernels (almonds, apples, apricots, cherries), electroplating, photography, fumigation, chemical synthesis.

Mechanism — Histotoxic Anoxia

CYANIDE = OXYGEN IS PRESENT, BUT CELLS CANNOT USE IT
Cyanide inhibits cytochrome oxidase (Fe3+) → cells cannot utilize O2 → ATP production stops → cellular hypoxia despite oxygen being present.

Clinical

Onset: seconds–minutes.

  1. Narrow arterial–venous O2 difference → venous blood remains bright red.
  2. Anion-gap metabolic acidosis.
  3. Lactate >10 mmol/L.

Other: bitter almond breath, bright pink/brick-red skin, dilated pupils, convulsions, opisthotonus, cardiovascular collapse, coma, respiratory arrest.

SubstanceFatal doseFatal period
HCN50–60 mg2–10 min for gas
NaCN/KCN200–300 mg~30 min for oral salts

Treatment

  1. Immediate support: airway, breathing, 100% O2.
  2. Decontamination: source notes gastric lavage with 5% sodium thiosulfate.
  3. Traditional kit: amyl nitrite inhalation (30 sec every minute) → sodium nitrite 3% solution, 10 mL IV over 10–20 min → methemoglobin formation → cyanide + methemoglobin → cyanmethemoglobin.
  4. Sodium thiosulfate: 25%, 50 mL IV → sulfur for rhodanese → cyanide → thiocyanate → urinary excretion.
  5. Modern preferred antidote: hydroxocobalamin (Cyanokit), 5 g IV → binds cyanide → cyanocobalamin (vitamin B12).
HYDROXOCOBALAMIN = preferred cyanide antidote.

Lee–Jones Test

Gastric aspirate + FeSO4 + NaOH → boil/cool → HCl. Positive: greenish-blue colour → cyanide.

PM

  • Bitter almond odor, especially on opening cranial cavity.
  • Bright pink/brick-red skin and postmortem patches.
  • Glistening eyes; widely dilated pupils.
  • Early + marked rigor mortis.
  • Bright-red fluid blood.
  • Stomach: eroded, blackened, corroded—especially with alkaline cyanides; due to alkaline hematin.
