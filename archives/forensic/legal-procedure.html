Courts in Nepal

A. District Court

  • Total number: 77
  • First court: For all cases (civil / criminal).
  • Jurisdiction: Within the district boundary.
  • Exception of jurisdiction: Women trafficking, cases under Civil Aviation Act, and Passport Act.
  • The Chief Justice shall, on recommendation of the Judicial Council, appoint judges of the district courts.
  • A district court shall have the power to originally try and settle all cases under its jurisdiction, to try petitions under law, including petitions of habeas corpus and prohibition, and hear appeals from decisions made by local level judicial bodies formed under state law.
Note: NMC Acts and Regulations are equivalent to district courts.
Contempt of court cases go to high court (i.e., those not attending court cases, any publicly criticised cases).

B. High Courts

  • Total number: 1 high court in each state (7 high courts total).
  • Composition: 1 Chief Judge or a judge of high court.
  • First court for:
    • Contempt of court cases.
    • Power to originally settle cases, hear appeals, and test judgements referred for confirmation.
    • Directed by laws.
    • Prolonged cases where complex legal questions exist.
  • The Chief Judge shall have the ultimate responsibility to make effective the administration of justice by the high court and subordinate courts/judicial bodies.
  • May issue orders, Writ cases (Habeas corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo-warranto).
  • Hears cases of appeal from district court of own jurisdiction.

C. Supreme Court

  • Court of record | Total number: 1
  • Composition: Maximum 20 judges + Chief Justice of Nepal.
  • Ordinary jurisdiction:
    • Appeals from high court.
    • All courts and judicial bodies are under the Supreme Court. Supreme Court shall have the final authority to interpret this constitution and laws.
    • Inspect, supervise, and give necessary directives to courts, specialized courts, or judicial bodies under its jurisdiction.
    • Contempt of court cases (own and undercourts).
  • Extraordinary jurisdiction:
    • Writ applications: Habeas corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo-warranto, Injunction.
    • Hearing to public litigation.
    • Precedent (Najir) laws made by Supreme Court.

D. Special Court

  • Trial of cases from Commission for Investigation of Abuse of Authority (CIAA).
  • Nepal Army Court.

Legal Writs Definitions

  • Writ: An order issued by a court requiring that something be done or giving authority to do a specified act.
  • Habeas corpus (Bandhi pratyakshikaran): Order to produce the prisoner at a designated time/place to determine legality of custody.
  • Mandamus (Parmadesh): Order to an inferior tribunal/individual to perform or refrain from performing a specific act.
  • Certiorari (Uttpreshan): Order to an inferior court to produce certified records of a case to inspect for errors/irregularities.
  • Prohibition (Pratisedh): Order stopping something from being done or used.
  • Quo-warranto (Adhikar preksha): Demanding by what authority or right an entity is taking action.
  • Injunction (Nisedhaagya): Court order to perform or restrain from performing a particular act.

Medicolegal System & Inquest

Functions of Medicolegal System

  1. To aid in the discovery and prosecution of crime: Investigation of death, abortion cases, sexual offenses, child abuse, and torture.
  2. To protect the innocent: Protects innocent individuals/doctors (e.g., protecting doctors whose patient died during treatment; protecting alleged assailant of sexual offense who is congenitally malformed).
  3. To protect public health: Study of infectious diseases, environmental conditions, and illicit drug abuse in causation of death. Helps identify mushroom poisoning, chronic arsenic poisoning, etc.
  4. To aid in administration of civil justice: Insurance and compensation claims effectively addressed through studying manner of death or injury.

Inquest Definition & Types

Inquest: Inquiry into the cause of death, where death has occurred in suspicious circumstances. In Nepal, Police Inquest exists.

Types of Inquest

  • A. Police Inquest: Incident of unnatural death (suicide, homicide, accident, neonaticide).
  • B. Magistrate Inquest: (Not done in Nepal). Done in cases of:
    • Death in police custody / interrogation
    • Death due to police firing
    • Death in prison / psychiatric hospital
    • Dowry deaths | Exhumation
    • Death/disappearance/rape alleged in police custody
  • C. Coroner's Inquest: Done in UK, USA, etc. Court of inquiry where jurors swear to give true verdict. Coroner has judicial powers.
  • D. Medical Examiner's Inquest: Done in USA, Japan, Canada. ME visits crime scene & conducts inquest (no judicial power). Superior because involved from beginning.

Police Inquest Procedure

Unnatural Death Occurs (Suicide, Homicide, etc.)

Key Personnel in Investigation: Witness • Technical Helpers (Medical personnel, forensic scientist, fingerprint expert, photographers) • Advocate for accused (Defense lawyer)

Problems / Current Situation in Nepal

  • No specific agency/institution to investigate human deaths. Investigations carried out by police and doctors at PHCs, district, regional, and zonal hospitals.
  • Law requires autopsies for "unnatural deaths", but no clear explanation of the term.
  • Physical facilities, supplies, minimal equipment, and assistants virtually non-existent.
  • Laboratory services are fragmented and do not approach minimal qualifications.

Medical Evidence & Court Examination

Medical Evidence Types 2080

Definition: All statements and documents in relation to matters of fact under inquiry, permitted or required by court.

  • A. Documentary Evidence:
    • Medical Certificates: Ill health, insanity, age, death, physical fitness.
    • Medicolegal Reports: Injury report, torture victim report, sexual assault report, drunkenness report, dying declaration.
  • B. Oral Evidence:
    • Direct: Witness directly experienced fact (e.g., saw defendant shoot victim).
    • Indirect (Circumstantial): Inference required (e.g., saw defendant with gun on that day).
  • C. Hearsay Evidence: Obtained from third party (e.g., friend told witness he saw the shooting).
  • D. Prima Facie Evidence: Apparent nature upon initial observation, presumed true unless disproved (e.g., videotape of death threats).
Note: Oral evidence is more important than documentary evidence because it allows cross-examination.

Recording Evidence in Court 2073

  • A. Oath: "I do swear in the name of god that what I shall state shall be the truth..." (Atheists "solemnly affirm").
  • B. Examination-in-Chief: First examination by lawyer who summoned witness. Elicits relevant medical facts. Leading questions NOT allowed.
  • C. Cross Examination: Questioned by opposite party (defense lawyer) to discredit witness. Leading questions ARE permitted. No time limit.
  • D. Re-examination: Conducted by original lawyer to clarify damaging details. No new matters allowed. Leading questions NOT allowed.
  • E. Question by Judge: Can be asked at any stage. Deposition signed by witness after reading.

Witness & Subpoena

  • Common Witness: Gives evidence on facts directly observed/perceived by own senses ("First-hand knowledge rule").
  • Expert Witness: Trained/skilled with specialized knowledge to draw opinions/conclusions from facts (e.g., Doctors, fingerprint experts).
  • Hostile Witness: Has interest in concealing truth, gives false evidence.
  • Perjury: Giving false evidence while under oath.
Medicolegal Role: A doctor acts as a common witness when describing wounds, and as an expert witness when giving opinions (e.g., antemortem vs. postmortem, cause of death).

Subpoena / Summons 2073

Document compelling attendance of witness in court under penalty on a specified date/time/place.

  • Failure to attend:
    • Civil case: Liable to pay damages.
    • Criminal case: Court may issue fine, imprisonment, or bailable/non-bailable warrants.
  • Conflicting Summons: Criminal court > Civil court; Higher court > Lower court; First received > Later received.

Conduct Money

Fee paid in civil cases at time of serving summons for expenses. In criminal cases, no fee is paid in advance, but conveyance/daily allowance can be claimed per govt rules.

Chain of Custody

Method to verify actual possession of object from identification to court evidence. Proper labeling, signatures, and receipts required. Shorter chain is better.

Nepal Medical Council Act, 2020 (1964)

Amending Acts: 2044 (1987), 2047 (1990), 2055 (1999), 2058 (2001).

Composition of NMC (Total 20 Members, 4-yr tenure):

  • Chairman: Nominated by Govt (min. 20 yrs exp, Master's degree).
  • Vice-Chairman: Registered doctor with min. 15 yrs exp.
  • Members (17 total):
    • 1 Dean/Rector of medical college
    • 1 Consumer representative
    • 8 Elected registered doctors
    • Chairman of NMA & Chairman of Nepal Dental Doctor Association
    • 2 Dental doctors (Master's degree)
    • 3 Medical doctors (Master's degree)
  • Registrar: Nominated by Govt of Nepal.

Functions of NMC 2072, 2068

  • Determine qualifications, conduct licensing exams (NMCLE), grant registration certificates, maintain register.
  • Recognize medical institutions and training programs.
  • Formulate curriculum, admission, and exam policies.
  • Establish Code of Conduct & ethical guidelines.
  • Disciplinary actions: Warning, Probation, Suspension of registration, Permanent removal ("Professional Death Sentence").

NMC Examination & Registration Process

  • NMCLE: Held 3 times/year. Requires min. 6 months internship. 180 MCQ questions (50% pass mark).
  • Provisional: For interns.
  • Temporary: Valid 2 years post-exam.
  • Permanent: Granted after 2 years experience.
  • Specialist & Foreign National Registrations: Separate categories.

NMC Code of Ethics Declaration (Summary)
