Courts in Nepal

A. District Court

  • Total number: 77
  • First court: For all cases (civil / criminal).
  • Jurisdiction: Within the district boundary.
  • Exception of jurisdiction: Women trafficking, cases under Civil Aviation Act, and Passport Act.
  • The Chief Justice shall, on recommendation of the Judicial Council, appoint judges of the district courts.
  • A district court shall have the power to originally try and settle all cases under its jurisdiction, to try petitions under law, including petitions of habeas corpus and prohibition, and hear appeals from decisions made by local level judicial bodies formed under state law.
Note: NMC Acts and Regulations are equivalent to district courts.
Contempt of court cases go to high court (i.e., those not attending court cases, any publicly criticised cases).

B. High Courts

  • Total number: 1 high court in each state (7 high courts total).
  • Composition: 1 Chief Judge or a judge of high court.
  • First court for:
    • Contempt of court cases.
    • Power to originally settle cases, hear appeals, and test judgements referred for confirmation.
    • Directed by laws.
    • Prolonged cases where complex legal questions exist.
  • The Chief Judge shall have the ultimate responsibility to make effective the administration of justice by the high court and subordinate courts/judicial bodies.
  • May issue orders, Writ cases (Habeas corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo-warranto).
  • Hears cases of appeal from district court of own jurisdiction.

C. Supreme Court

  • Court of record | Total number: 1
  • Composition: Maximum 20 judges + Chief Justice of Nepal.
  • Ordinary jurisdiction:
    • Appeals from high court.
    • All courts and judicial bodies are under the Supreme Court. Supreme Court shall have the final authority to interpret this constitution and laws.
    • Inspect, supervise, and give necessary directives to courts, specialized courts, or judicial bodies under its jurisdiction.
    • Contempt of court cases (own and undercourts).
  • Extraordinary jurisdiction:
    • Writ applications: Habeas corpus, Mandamus, Certiorari, Prohibition, Quo-warranto, Injunction.
    • Hearing to public litigation.
    • Precedent (Najir) laws made by Supreme Court.

D. Special Court

  • Trial of cases from Commission for Investigation of Abuse of Authority (CIAA).
  • Nepal Army Court.

Legal Writs Definitions

  • Writ: An order issued by a court requiring that something be done or giving authority to do a specified act.
  • Habeas corpus (Bandhi pratyakshikaran): Order to produce the prisoner at a designated time/place to determine legality of custody.
  • Mandamus (Parmadesh): Order to an inferior tribunal/individual to perform or refrain from performing a specific act.
  • Certiorari (Uttpreshan): Order to an inferior court to produce certified records of a case to inspect for errors/irregularities.
  • Prohibition (Pratisedh): Order stopping something from being done or used.
  • Quo-warranto (Adhikar preksha): Demanding by what authority or right an entity is taking action.
  • Injunction (Nisedhaagya): Court order to perform or restrain from performing a particular act.

Medicolegal System & Inquest

Functions of Medicolegal System

  1. To aid in the discovery and prosecution of crime: Investigation of death, abortion cases, sexual offenses, child abuse, and torture.
  2. To protect the innocent: Protects innocent individuals/doctors (e.g., protecting doctors whose patient died during treatment; protecting alleged assailant of sexual offense who is congenitally malformed).
  3. To protect public health: Study of infectious diseases, environmental conditions, and illicit drug abuse in causation of death. Helps identify mushroom poisoning, chronic arsenic poisoning, etc.
  4. To aid in administration of civil justice: Insurance and compensation claims effectively addressed through studying manner of death or injury.

Inquest Definition & Types

Inquest: Inquiry into the cause of death, where death has occurred in suspicious circumstances. In Nepal, Police Inquest exists.

Types of Inquest

  • A. Police Inquest: Incident of unnatural death (suicide, homicide, accident, neonaticide).
  • B. Magistrate Inquest: (Not done in Nepal). Done in cases of:
    • Death in police custody / interrogation
    • Death due to police firing
    • Death in prison / psychiatric hospital
    • Dowry deaths | Exhumation
    • Death/disappearance/rape alleged in police custody
  • C. Coroner's Inquest: Done in UK, USA, etc. Court of inquiry where jurors swear to give true verdict. Coroner has judicial powers.
  • D. Medical Examiner's Inquest: Done in USA, Japan, Canada. ME visits crime scene & conducts inquest (no judicial power). Superior because involved from beginning.

Key Personnel in Investigation: Witness • Technical Helpers (Medical personnel, forensic scientist, fingerprint expert, photographers) • Advocate for accused (Defense lawyer)

Problems / Current Situation in Nepal

  • No specific agency/institution to investigate human deaths. Investigations carried out by police and doctors at PHCs, district, regional, and zonal hospitals.
  • Law requires autopsies for "unnatural deaths", but no clear explanation of the term.
  • Physical facilities, supplies, minimal equipment, and assistants virtually non-existent.
  • Laboratory services are fragmented and do not approach minimal qualifications.

Medical Evidence & Court Examination

Medical Evidence Types 2080

Definition: All statements and documents in relation to matters of fact under inquiry, permitted or required by court.

  • A. Documentary Evidence:
    • Medical Certificates: Ill health, insanity, age, death, physical fitness.
    • Medicolegal Reports: Injury report, torture victim report, sexual assault report, drunkenness report, dying declaration.
  • B. Oral Evidence:
    • Direct: Witness directly experienced fact (e.g., saw defendant shoot victim).
    • Indirect (Circumstantial): Inference required (e.g., saw defendant with gun on that day).
  • C. Hearsay Evidence: Obtained from third party (e.g., friend told witness he saw the shooting).
  • D. Prima Facie Evidence: Apparent nature upon initial observation, presumed true unless disproved (e.g., videotape of death threats).
Note: Oral evidence is more important than documentary evidence because it allows cross-examination.

Recording Evidence in Court 2073

  • A. Oath: "I do swear in the name of god that what I shall state shall be the truth..." (Atheists "solemnly affirm").
  • B. Examination-in-Chief: First examination by lawyer who summoned witness. Elicits relevant medical facts. Leading questions NOT allowed.
  • C. Cross Examination: Questioned by opposite party (defense lawyer) to discredit witness. Leading questions ARE permitted. No time limit.
  • D. Re-examination: Conducted by original lawyer to clarify damaging details. No new matters allowed. Leading questions NOT allowed.
  • E. Question by Judge: Can be asked at any stage. Deposition signed by witness after reading.

WITNESS

Definition: A witness is a person who has seen, perceived or examined a fact or about fact and gives evidences in the court relating to the matter of facts under inquiry.

Types:

  • Common witness (witness of fact or occurrence witness): 2065, 2055
    Definition: A person who gives evidence on facts observed or perceived by him.
    He must show that he was able to perceive it by one of his own senses and he had actually observed this. Called as "first hand knowledge rule"
    Eg. A person saw the defendant stab some person, a person who witnessed an accident etc.
  • Expert witness: 2073, 2065, 2055
    Definition: He is a person who has been trained or is skilled or has knowledge, experience or education in technical or scientific subject and capable of drawing opinions and conclusions from the facts observed by himself or noticed by others.
    Eg. Doctors, firearms expert, fingerprint expert etc.
  • Hostile witness: Has some interest in concealing a part of truth and so willingly gives false evidence.
  • Perjury: On oath, a person gives false evidence.
Note: A doctor can be both, a common witness and an expert witness.
  • When he describes wounds on a body, he acts as a common witness.
  • When he says that wounds are antemortem or postmortem, or they are suicidal, homicidal or accidental and gives opinion regarding cause of death, he acts as an expert witness.

Subpoena or Summons 2073

  • It is a document compelling the attendance of a witness in a court of law under penalty, on a particular day, time and place, for the purpose of giving evidence.
  • Witness has to bring with him all the required evidences.
  • It is issued by the court, bears the sign of the presiding officer of the court and bears the seal of court.
  • After receiving the summon, the receiver i.e. witness has to sign on the back of another copy.
  • Summon must be obeyed.
  • If the witness fails to attend the court:
    • In civil case: He will be liable to pay damages.
    • In criminal case: Court will issue notice and after hearing if it finds the witness to be negligent without justification, may punish him with fine or imprisonment or may issue bailable or non-bailable warrants.
  • If a witness is summoned by two courts at same time then:
    • Attend criminal court and inform civil court.
    • Attend higher court and inform lower court.
    • Attend the court which summoned first and inform the later, if court of same status summon. Attend the second court after finishing the first.

Conduct Money

  • It is the fee offered or paid to a witness in civil cases, at the time of serving the summons to meet the expenses for attending the court.
  • Doctor can express his dissatisfaction regarding the amount before giving evidence.
  • In criminal cases, no fee is paid to the witness. He must attend the court.
  • However, conveyance charges and daily allowance can be asked as per the government rules.

Chain of Custody

  • Definition: A method to verify actual possession of the object from the time it was first identified until it is offered as evidence in the court.
  • Proper labeling with victim's name, time and date, nature of specimen, identification number and signature of doctor.
  • A receipt must be obtained by the one handing it over to the other.
  • Shorter the chain, the better.

Composition of Nepal Medical Council:

(duration 4 years)
Total number of members = 20

  • CHAIRMAN: One person nominated by Government of Nepal, from amongst doctors having a min. 20 yrs. Experience as registered doctors with master degree in medical science.
  • VICE-CHAIRMAN: One person amongst registered medical doctor with min. 15 Yrs. Experience working as registered doctor.
  • MEMBERS: 2068
    • One person nominated by Government of Nepal, from amongst the Dean or rectors of the medical colleges or health science academy.
    • One person nominated by Government of Nepal, from amongst consumer.
    • Eight persons elected form amongst the registered doctors.
    • Chairman, Nepal Medical Association (NMA).
    • Chairman, Nepal Dental Doctor Association.
    • Two persons nominated by Government of Nepal, form amongst dental doctors with min. master degree.
    • Three person nominated by Government of Nepal form amongst medical doctors with min. master degree.
  • REGISTRAR: One person nominated by Government of Nepal for the post of registrar.

Function (NMC) 2072, 2068

  • To determine the qualification of doctors and to provide registration certificate by taking licensing examination for new doctors as well as to maintain the register.
  • To give recognition to medical institutions for providing formal studies in medical science and training.
  • To formulate policies related to curriculum, admission, term and examination system of teaching institute of medical education and to make recommendation for cancellation of registration and approved by renewing and evaluating such system/procedure.
  • To formulate necessary policies and to make Code of Conduct to run doctors profession smoothly.
  • Active participation in making National Health Policy.
  • To maintain the register.
  • To take action against the doctors who violate ethical norms and regulations: 2069
    1. Warning
    2. Keeping under supervision of senior doctor (under probation)
    3. Suspension of registration
    4. Permanent removal of registration (Professional Death Sentence)

Process for NMC Examination

NMC takes Nepal medial council license examination (NMCLE) 3 times in a year. Every new graduates, be it from Nepal or abroad, has to participate in it.

Criteria for participation are at least 6 months of internship in NMC authorized hospital or medical college. Candidates will have to secure 50% score in a MCQs type paper with a total of 180 questions. Results get published normally on the same day evening or late night. Passed candidates get a temporary NMC number for 2 years which will be replaced by permanent NMC number and certificate after 2 years of experience.

Types of Registration Provided by NMC

  • Provisional (for interns)
  • Temporary (after passing out licensing exam)
  • Permanent (after 2yrs of experience)
    Same registration number of temporary registration is provided to permanent registration.
  • Specialist registration
    A specialist in radiology, anesthesia, dermatology, etc. A different registration number is given.
  • Registration for foreign national
    It requires justification of the need and Annual renew of the license. Only interview is taken and not a written exam.

NMC Code of Ethics Declaration:

The following declaration should be read and agreed upon by the applicant at the time of registration:

  • Solemnly pledge myself to dedicate my life to the service of humanity.
  • Even under threat and duress I will not use my knowledge contrary to the norms of humanity.
  • I will maintain the utmost respect for human life right from the time of conception as per the laws of the land.
  • I will not allow consideration of age, sex, religion, nationality, ethnicity, politics, or social standing to intervene between my duty and my patient.
  • I will carry out my professional duties with conscience and dignity.
  • The health of my patient will be my first consideration.
  • I will respect the secrets of my patients confided in me.
  • I will give to my teachers the respect and gratitude that is their due.
  • I will maintain, by all means in my power, the honour and noble traditions of the medical profession.
  • I will maintain utmost rapport with my professional colleagues. I make these promises solemnly, freely and upon my honour.
