Toxicology: Inebriant & Depressant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 574–582)

1. Ethyl Alcohol (Ethanol)

Definition

  • Hydroxyl derivative of an aliphatic hydrocarbon.
  • Alcohols are classified into:
    • Monohydroxy alcohols
    • Dihydroxy alcohols
    • Trihydroxy alcohols
  • Ethanol (Grain alcohol) is a:
    • Colorless
    • Transparent
    • Volatile liquid
    • Spirituous odor
    • Burning taste
  • Produced by fermentation of sugars by yeast.

High-Yield Definitions

  • Absolute alcohol → 99.95% ethanol.
  • Rectified spirit → 90% ethanol.
  • Proof spirit
    • At 10.5°C, weighs exactly 12/13 the weight of an equal volume of distilled water.
    • Proof = 2 × % alcohol by volume.
  • 1 Unit of alcohol = 8 g ethanol.
  • Arrack
    • Prepared from palm, rice, sugar or jaggery.
    • Alcohol content: 40–50%.

Pharmacokinetics

1. Absorption

  • Absorbed by simple diffusion from:
    • Mouth
    • Esophagus
    • Stomach
    • Small intestine
  • Carbonated drinks → Increase absorption.
  • Food → Delays absorption.
  • Peak blood concentration occurs in 45–90 minutes.

2. Distribution

  • Distributed to almost all tissues.
  • Not distributed significantly to:
    • Bone
    • Adipose tissue
  • Important Ratios
    • Blood : Urine = 1 : 1.3
    • Blood : Breath = 1 : 2100
    • Blood : Saliva = 1 : 1.2

3. Metabolism

  • 90% metabolized in the liver.
  • 10% excreted unchanged through lungs, kidneys and sweat.
  • Main metabolic pathways:
    • Alcohol dehydrogenase (Major pathway)
    • MEOS (Microsomal Ethanol Oxidizing System)
    • Catalase-peroxidase pathway
Metabolic Pathway

Alcohol → Acetaldehyde → Acetic Acid / Acetyl-CoA → Krebs Cycle → CO₂ + H₂O

Mechanism of Action

  • Enhances GABA-mediated inhibitory neurotransmission.
  • Acts on NMDA (glutamate) receptors.
  • Produces CNS depression by increasing inhibitory neurotransmission.

Systemic Effects

Central Nervous System (CNS)

  • Low dose → Apparent stimulation (due to depression of inhibitory centers).
  • High dose → General CNS depression.
  • Large doses depress:
    • Respiratory center
    • Vasomotor center

Cardiovascular System

  • Small dose
    • Cutaneous vasodilation
    • Gastric vasodilation
  • Moderate dose
    • Tachycardia
    • Mild rise in blood pressure
  • Large dose
    • Myocardial depression
    • Vasomotor depression
  • Chronic alcoholism causes:
    • Hypertension
    • Cardiomyopathy
    • Atrial fibrillation
    • QT prolongation

Blood

  • Small amounts:
    • ↑ HDL
    • ↓ LDL
  • >3 drinks/day → Increased CAD risk.
  • Causes megaloblastic anemia by interfering with folate metabolism.

Other Effects

  • Produces false sensation of warmth.
  • High doses impair temperature regulation.
  • Causes diuresis by inhibiting ADH.
  • High concentrations cause erosive gastritis.

Alcohol Content of Common Beverages

Beverage Alcohol (%)
Beer 4–8%
Wine 10–20%
Whisky / Gin / Brandy 40–45%
Rum 50%

Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) & Clinical Effects

BAC (mg%) Stage Important Features
0–50 Sober
  • No significant effects
  • Mild euphoria
50–100 Excitement
  • Disinhibition
  • ↑ Self-confidence
  • Impaired judgment
  • Slurred speech
  • Nystagmus
  • Mild incoordination
100–150 Incoordination
  • Mental confusion
  • Loss of memory
  • Slow reaction time
  • Poor judgment
150–300 Intoxication / Early Stupor
  • Ataxia
  • Staggering gait
  • Marked confusion
  • Drowsiness
  • Thick speech
300–400 Stupor
  • Marked depression
  • Poor response to stimuli
  • Possible coma
>400 Coma & Death
  • Deep coma
  • Respiratory failure
  • Loss of reflexes
  • Death

Last-Minute Viva Pearls

  • Absolute alcohol = 99.95%
  • Rectified spirit = 90%
  • 1 unit alcohol = 8 g
  • Peak absorption = 45–90 min
  • 90% metabolized in liver
  • Blood : Breath = 1 : 2100 ⭐
  • ADH inhibition → Diuresis
  • Low dose → Apparent CNS stimulation
  • High dose → CNS depression
  • >400 mg% BAC → Coma & Death

Important Clinical Signs of Alcohol Intoxication

  • Alcohol Gaze Nystagmus (AGN)
    • Jerky eye movement in the direction of gaze.
    • Independent of head position.
    • Appears at BAC 40–100 mg% (average 80 mg%).
  • McEwan Sign
    • Pupils are constricted.
    • Painful stimulus causes:
      • Slow dilatation
      • Followed by slow constriction
    • Seen when BAC ≥300 mg%.
  • Romberg Sign
    • Unable to stand steadily with:
      • Feet together
      • Eyes closed

The 4 "M's" of Alcoholism

  • Micturition Syncope
    • Transient loss of consciousness while passing urine.
    • Usually after heavy beer intake.
  • Munich Beer Heart
    • Cardiac dilatation and hypertrophy.
    • Occurs in chronic heavy beer drinkers.
  • Morbid Jealousy (Othello Syndrome)
    • Delusion that spouse/partner is unfaithful.
  • Mickey Finn
    • Alcohol + Chloral hydrate.
    • Used as "knock-out drops."

Acute Alcohol Poisoning

  • Fatal Dose
    • 150–250 mL absolute alcohol within 1 hour.
  • Fatal Period
    • 12–24 hours.

Treatment

  1. Gastric lavage with alkaline solution.
  2. Keep the patient warm.
  3. IV fluids:
    • 1 L Normal Saline
    • 10% Glucose
    • Thiamine 100 mg
    • Insulin 15 units
  4. Oxygen therapy.
  5. Artificial ventilation if required.
  6. Hemodialysis / Peritoneal dialysis in deep coma.

Treatment of Chronic Alcoholism

  • Disulfiram
    • 250 mg/day
    • Inhibits Aldehyde Dehydrogenase (ALDH).
  • Calcium Carbimide
    • 50 mg/day
  • Supportive therapy:
    • Chlorpromazine
    • Clonidine
    • Chlormethiazole
    • Psychotherapy
    • Alcoholics Anonymous (AA)

Autopsy Findings

Acute Alcohol Poisoning
  • Alcoholic odor on opening body cavities.
  • Acute gastritis with thick mucus.
  • Congestion of:
    • Brain
    • Lungs
    • Liver
  • Dark fluid blood.
  • Decomposition delayed.
Chronic Alcoholism
  • Malnutrition.
  • Reddish-brown gastric mucosa.
  • Fatty liver / Cirrhosis.
  • Dilated heart with fatty degeneration.
  • Testicular atrophy.
  • Cerebellar degeneration.

2. Clinical Examination of Drunkenness

Definition

Drunkenness is a condition in which alcohol has impaired a person's physical or mental faculties sufficiently that they are unable to safely perform the activity or occupation they were engaged in.

Clinical Examination

  1. Record exact date and time of examination.
  2. Exclude medical conditions that mimic intoxication
    • Hypoglycemia
    • Stroke
    • Epilepsy
    • Neurological disease
  3. Assess:
    • Appearance & dress
    • Behavior
    • Speech
    • Memory
    • Judgment
    • Self-control
    • Handwriting
    • Reaction time
  4. Physical signs:
    • Alcoholic breath
    • Bounding pulse
    • Dry/furred tongue
    • Congested eyelids
    • Nystagmus
    • Negative convergence test
  5. Coordination tests:
    • Finger–nose test
    • Heel–shin test
    • Pick up small objects
    • Light a match
    • Button/unbutton shirt or coat

Widmark Formula

a = p × r × c

  • a = Total alcohol in body (g)
  • p = Body weight (kg)
  • r = Distribution factor
    • Male = 0.68
    • Female = 0.55
  • c = Blood alcohol concentration

Urine Formula

a = ¾ × p × r × q

Henry's Law (Breath Analysis)

  • At 34°C, alcohol concentration in alveolar air and blood remains constant.
  • 2100–2300 mL alveolar air ≈ 1 mL blood.
  • Basis of Breathalyzer testing.

3. Complications of Chronic Alcoholism

  • Delirium Tremens (DT)
    • Occurs 72–96 hours after the last drink.
    • Features:
      • Coarse tremors
      • Insomnia
      • Agitation
      • Visual & auditory hallucinations
      • Seeing crawling insects/snakes (formication)
      • Autonomic overactivity
    • Mortality: 5–15%
  • Wernicke Encephalopathy
    • Due to Vitamin B₁ (Thiamine) deficiency.
    • Classic Triad (GOA)
      • G → Global confusion
      • O → Ophthalmoplegia
      • A → Ataxia
  • Korsakoff Psychosis
    • Chronic thiamine deficiency.
    • Features:
      • Severe amnesia
      • Confabulation
  • Marchiafava-Bignami Syndrome
    • Degeneration of the corpus callosum.
  • Mallory-Weiss Syndrome
    • Mucosal tear at the gastroesophageal junction.
    • Occurs after repeated severe vomiting/retching.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls

  • AGN → BAC 40–100 mg%
  • McEwan Sign → BAC ≥300 mg%
  • Romberg → Unable to stand with eyes closed
  • Fatal dose = 150–250 mL absolute alcohol
  • Fatal period = 12–24 hrs
  • Disulfiram inhibits Aldehyde Dehydrogenase
  • Widmark constant: Male 0.68 • Female 0.55
  • Henry's Law → Breath : Blood = 2100–2300 : 1
  • DT → 72–96 hrs after last drink
  • Wernicke Triad = GOA (Global confusion • Ophthalmoplegia • Ataxia)
  • Korsakoff = Amnesia + Confabulation

4. Methyl Alcohol (Methanol)

Definition

  • Also called:
    • Wood alcohol
    • Carbinol
  • Colorless, volatile liquid.
  • Odor resembles ethanol.
  • Toxicity is mainly due to its metabolites:
    • Formaldehyde
    • Formic acid

High-Yield Facts

  • Fatal Dose: 60–200 mL
  • Fatal Period: 24–36 hours (may be delayed up to 2–4 days)
  • Target Organ: Optic nerve
  • Toxic metabolite: Formic acid
  • Classical complication: Permanent blindness

Clinical Features

  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Severe abdominal cramps
  • Headache with neck stiffness
  • Vertigo
  • Metabolic acidosis
  • Hypothermia
  • Convulsions
  • Coma

Ophthalmic Features

  • Blurred vision ("looking through a snow field")
  • Fixed dilated pupils
  • Optic neuritis
  • Optic disc hyperemia
  • Permanent blindness

Laboratory Findings

  • High anion gap metabolic acidosis
  • Hypophosphatemia
  • Raised CPK
  • Raised amylase
  • Blood methanol >50 mg/100 mL → Severe poisoning

Treatment

  1. Gastric lavage with 5% Sodium bicarbonate.
  2. Activated charcoal.
  3. Antidote: Ethanol
    • 10% Ethanol IV
    • 10 mL/kg over 30 minutes
    • Maintenance: 1.5 mL/kg/hour
    • Acts by competitive inhibition of Alcohol Dehydrogenase.
  4. Antidote: Fomepizole (4-MP)
    • 20 mg/kg
    • Given over 5 days (as per protocol).
  5. Correct metabolic acidosis with IV Sodium bicarbonate.
  6. Give Folinic acid to enhance formic acid metabolism.
  7. Hemodialysis is the treatment of choice in severe poisoning.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls

  • Methanol → Formic acid → Optic nerve toxicity
  • Most characteristic feature = Blindness
  • High anion gap metabolic acidosis
  • Antidotes = Ethanol or Fomepizole
  • Severe poisoning → Hemodialysis

5. Ethylene Glycol

Definition

  • Colorless, odorless, non-volatile liquid.
  • Bitter-sweet taste.
  • Found in:
    • Antifreeze
    • Radiator coolants
    • Brake fluids
  • Metabolized to:
    • Glycoaldehyde
    • Glycolic acid
    • Oxalic acid

High-Yield Facts

  • Fatal Dose: 100–200 mL
  • Fatal Period: ~3 days
  • Main target organ: Kidney
  • Toxic metabolite: Oxalic acid

Clinical Phases

Phase 1 (0–12 hours) — CNS Phase

  • Vomiting
  • Lethargy
  • Ataxia
  • Convulsions
  • Coma

Phase 2 (12–24 hours) — Cardiopulmonary Phase

  • Tachycardia
  • Tachypnea
  • Circulatory failure

Phase 3 (24–72 hours) — Renal Phase

  • Flank pain
  • Acute tubular necrosis
  • Acute renal failure
  • Calcium oxalate crystalluria

Diagnosis

  • Urine shows calcium oxalate crystals.
  • Urine fluoresces under a Wood's lamp if fluorescent antifreeze was ingested.
  • High anion gap metabolic acidosis.

Treatment

  • Antidote:
    • Ethanol
    • Fomepizole
  • Correct acidosis with IV sodium bicarbonate.
  • Hemodialysis for severe poisoning.
  • Supportive management.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls

  • Methanol → Formic acid → Blindness
  • Ethylene glycol → Oxalic acid → Kidney failure
  • Methanol target = Optic nerve
  • Ethylene glycol target = Kidney
  • Ethylene glycol urine → Calcium oxalate crystals
  • Antidotes for BOTH = Ethanol or Fomepizole
  • Severe poisoning → Hemodialysis

6. Volatile Substance Abuse (Inhalants)

Definition

  • Deliberate inhalation of volatile chemicals for their psychoactive effects.
  • Common inhalants include:
    • Toluene
    • Gasoline (Petrol)
    • Xylene
    • Benzene
    • Fluorocarbons

Methods of Inhalation

  • Huffing
    • Inhaling vapors from a solvent-soaked cloth held over the nose and mouth.
  • Bagging
    • Breathing in and out from a plastic or paper bag containing volatile substances.
  • Sniffing
    • Direct inhalation from the container or nozzle.

Causes of Death

  • Sudden cardiac death (fatal arrhythmias)
  • Hypoxia
  • Plastic bag asphyxia
  • Reflex cardiac arrest

Autopsy Findings

  • Excoriation around the nose and mouth.
  • Congestion and damage of visceral organs.
  • Characteristic solvent odor may be present.

Specimens for Chemical Analysis

  • Clothing
  • Blood
  • Fat
  • Brain
  • Lungs

Last-Minute Viva Pearls

  • Huffing → Cloth
  • Bagging → Plastic bag
  • Sniffing → Direct container
  • Commonest cause of death → Fatal arrhythmia (Sudden sniffing death)

7. Barbiturate Poisoning

Definition

  • Derivatives of barbituric acid.
  • Produce CNS depression by:
    • Enhancing GABA-A receptor activity.
    • Increasing chloride (Cl⁻) influx.
    • Depressing neuronal activity and central sympathetic outflow.

Classification

Type Examples Fatal Dose
Ultra-short acting Thiopental, Methohexital —
Short acting Pentobarbital, Secobarbital 1–2 g
Intermediate acting Amobarbital, Aprobarbital 2–3 g
Long acting Phenobarbital, Mephobarbital 3–5 g

Clinical Features

Barbiturate Triad

  • Apnea
  • Cold skin
  • Hypotension

Other Features

  • Slurred speech
  • Ataxia
  • Nystagmus
  • Constricted pupils
  • Barbiturate blisters (bullous lesions over pressure points)
  • Oliguria ("Liquid Gold Urine")
  • Respiratory depression
  • Coma

Treatment

  • Supportive care (Airway, Breathing, Circulation).
  • Coma Cocktail
    • 50% Dextrose — 100 mL
    • Thiamine — 100 mg
    • Naloxone — 2 mg
  • Forced alkaline diuresis
    • Useful mainly for Phenobarbital.
  • Charcoal hemoperfusion in severe poisoning.
  • No specific antidote.

Autopsy Findings

  • Tablets/capsules/powder in stomach and intestine.
  • Frothy secretions from nose and mouth.
  • Barbiturate blisters.
  • Congestion of internal organs.
  • Peripheral cyanosis.

Barbiturate Automatism

  • Patient takes one dose of barbiturate.
  • Develops toxic delirium or amnesia.
  • Forgets that the medicine was already taken.
  • Repeatedly consumes additional doses.
  • Results in accidental fatal overdose.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls

  • Mechanism → ↑ GABA-A → ↑ Cl⁻ influx
  • Triad = Apnea • Cold Skin • Hypotension
  • Barbiturate Blisters ⭐
  • "Liquid Gold Urine" = Oliguria
  • Forced alkaline diuresis → Phenobarbital
  • No specific antidote
  • Coma cocktail = Dextrose + Thiamine + Naloxone
  • Barbiturate automatism = Repeated dosing due to amnesia
