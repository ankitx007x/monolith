Non-Metallic Irritant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 555–558)

PHOSPHORUS

Phosphorus exists in two main allotropic forms: White (yellow) phosphorus and Red (amorphous) phosphorus.

Comparison: White vs Red Phosphorus

Trait White Phosphorus Red Phosphorus
Colour White or yellow Reddish-brown
Appearance Translucent, waxy cylinder Amorphous, solid mass
Smell Garlicky Odourless
Taste Garlicky Tasteless
Luminosity Luminous in dark Non-luminous
Exposure to Air Oxidises and emits white fumes; ignites at 34°C (kept under water) Non-oxidised, non-fuming, non-inflammable
Uses Fertilizers, vermin-killers, rodenticides, fireworks, gunpowder On the sides of matchboxes
Toxicity Highly toxic Non-toxic

Mechanism of Action

It acts as a protoplasmic poison that directly affects cellular oxidation, leading to severe hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, and cardiac toxicity.

Clinical Features

1. Fulminating Poisoning

  • Occurs when more than 1 g is ingested.
  • Death occurs within 12 hours due to shock and cardiovascular collapse caused by direct toxicity to the heart and blood vessels.
  • Patients who survive longer than 12 hours become restless, delirious, and maniacal before death.
  • Manifests with intense thirst, severe nausea, vomiting, and retching.

2. Acute Poisoning

Follows a characteristic three-stage clinical course:

  • First Stage (Within hours to 3 days):
    • Skin contact produces painful, penetrating 2nd and 3rd-degree burns.
    • Burning pain in the throat and abdomen with intense thirst, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.
    • Breath, vomitus, and feces have a garlic-like odor.
    • Diagnostic Hallmark: Luminescent "smoking" vomitus and feces (smoky stool syndrome).
  • Second Stage: Symptom-free latent period lasting 2 to 3 days.
  • Third Stage: Multi-organ failure and systemic collapse.
System Signs & Symptoms
GIT Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, hematemesis
Hepatic Tender hepatomegaly, jaundice, pruritus, acute yellow atrophy
Renal Oliguria, hematuria, casts, albuminuria, anuria, renal insufficiency
CNS Restlessness, anxiety, insomnia, headache, confusion, hallucinations, convulsions, delirium, coma
Important Clinical Pearls
  • Luminescent, smoky vomitus and stool are pathognomonic for white phosphorus ingestion.
  • Death typically results from shock, acute hepatic failure, or CNS damage.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 60–120 mg of white phosphorus.
  • Fatal Period: Within 24 hours (may be delayed to 5–7 days).

Treatment

  1. Gastric Lavage: Using 1:5000 solution of KMnO4 (chemical antidote), which oxidizes phosphorus into harmless phosphoric acid and phosphates.
  2. Activated Charcoal: Administered to adsorb remaining poison.
  3. Copper Sulphate Wash: Lavage with 0.2% CuSO4 solution (or 0.2 g every 5 minutes until emesis) to form a protective coating of copper phosphide. Use with caution due to risk of acute copper toxicity.
  4. Vitamin K: 20 mg IV in repeated doses to combat hypoprothrombinemia and bleeding.
  5. Bowel Evacuation: Magnesium sulfate purges; repeated at intervals for several days.
  6. Dietary Restriction: Strictly avoid oils and fats as they dissolve phosphorus and enhance GI absorption.
  7. Fluid & Electrolyte Therapy: Glucose-saline, plasma transfusion, and noradrenaline to protect liver and manage shock/dehydration.
  8. Renal Support: Hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis for acute renal failure.
  9. Dermal Burns: Wash thoroughly with 1% copper sulfate solution in water.

Post-Mortem Findings

External Findings

  • Emaciation, purpuric hemorrhages on skin, jaundice, and garlic odor.
  • Oral mucous membranes show corrosion.
  • Post-mortem lividity/hypostasis is dark brown in color.

Internal Findings

  • GI Tract: Mucous membranes yellow or grayish-white, softened, thickened, and inflamed. Garlic odor and luminous material may be present in dark examination.
  • Hemorrhages: Multiple petechiae in muscles, serosa, mucosal membranes, liver, kidneys, endocardium, pericardium, epicardium, peritoneum, lungs, and brain.
  • Liver: Swollen, yellow, soft, greasy, and easily ruptured. Shows acute yellow atrophy in patients surviving >1 week.
  • Kidneys: Enlarged, greasy, yellow, with mucosal surface hemorrhages.
  • Heart: Flabby, pale, with severe fatty degeneration.
  • Lungs: Fat emboli found in pulmonary arterioles and capillaries.
  • Blood: Dark, tarry appearance with impaired coagulability.

Chronic Phosphorus Poisoning

Caused by chronic inhalation of phosphorus fumes over several years in industrial settings.

Phossy Jaw (Glass Jaw)

  • Fumes enter through a carious tooth or alveolar interspace.
  • Initial toothache followed by jaw swelling, tooth loosening, gum necrosis, and bone sequestration in the mandible.
  • Leads to suppurative osteomyelitis and necrosis of the jaw with multiple discharging sinuses producing foul-smelling pus.

Systemic Features

  • Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, abdominal pain, indigestion, purging.
  • Joint pain, weight loss, bronchitis, jaundice, anemia, bone marrow suppression.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Accidental: Children chewing fireworks or rat paste.
  • Homicidal: Mixed in alcohol or coffee to disguise smell/taste. Symptoms mimic acute liver disease.
  • Abortifacient: Taken orally or inserted vaginally to procure illegal abortion.
  • Military / War: Exposure via grenade fragments, smoke screens, or incendiary bullets.
  • Arson: White phosphorus wrapped in wet dung/cloth thrown onto roofs; ignites upon drying.

IODINE

Iodine exists as bluish-black, soft, scaly crystals with a metallic luster, unpleasant taste, and gives off characteristic violet-colored vapor at all temperatures.

Mechanism of Action

  • Protoplasmic poison that fixes cellular proteins and causes tissue necrosis.
  • Direct irritant to respiratory passages, skin, and mucosal surfaces.
  • Binds bicarbonate ions, precipitating severe metabolic acidosis and acute renal failure.

Clinical Features

Acute Poisoning

  • Inhalation: Glottic edema, upper respiratory tract irritation, pulmonary edema, and congestion.
  • Ingestion:
    • Acts as an acid corrosive poison.
    • Burning pain extending from mouth to stomach, intense thirst, salivation.
    • Vomiting and purging; vomitus and excreta are dark yellow or blue (if starch present) with blood and iodine odor.
    • Lips and angles of mouth stained brown/yellowish-brown.
    • Giddiness, limb cramps, fainting, weak pulse, cold/clammy skin.
    • Renal: Scanty red-brown urine, albuminuria, nephritis, and acute renal failure.
    • Metabolic: Severe metabolic acidosis.
Tetrad of Iodine Poisoning
  • Red urine
  • Iodine mumps (parotid swelling)
  • Ioderma (skin eruptions)
  • Yellow skin & GI mucosa

Chronic Poisoning (Iodism)

  • Catarrhal symptoms: Frontal sinus pain, rhinorrhea, conjunctivitis, bronchial catarrh, excessive salivation.
  • GI: Nausea, vomiting, purging, emaciation.
  • Iodine Mumps: Bilateral parotid gland swelling.
  • Glandular atrophy: Wasting of breasts, testes, and lymph nodes.
  • Ioderma: Acneiform or erythematous skin patches, urticaria.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 2 to 4 g.
  • Fatal Period: 24 hours.

Treatment

  1. Gastric Lavage: Wash stomach with warm water containing soluble starch or albumen (forms insoluble blue starch-iodide complex).
  2. Chemical Antidote: 1% to 5% Sodium Thiosulfate solution converts iodine to harmless iodide.
  3. Excretion Promotion: Administration of Sodium Chloride (NaCl) promotes urinary excretion of iodide.
  4. Demulcents: Arrowroot, barley water, and alkalis.
  5. Adsorbent: Activated charcoal to bind free iodine.
  6. Dermal Lesions: Wash affected skin with 20% alcohol solution.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • Skin, lips, oral mucosa, and clothes show yellowish-brown stains.
  • Gastric mucosa is inflamed, excoriated, and stained yellowish-brown (appears blue if starch is present in stomach).
  • Characteristic iodine odor upon opening body cavities.
  • Generalized internal organ congestion.
  • Fatty degeneration of heart, liver, and kidneys; cerebral edema.

Memory Booster

General Rule: If the fatal dose is in mg, the fatal period is in hours; if the fatal dose is in grams, the fatal period is in days.
Non-Metallic Irritant Fatal Dose Fatal Period Key High-Yield Pearls
Phosphorus 60–120 mg 4–10 hours (or up to 5–7 days) Garlic odor, luminescent "smoking" stool, Phossy jaw, fatty liver degeneration. Avoid dietary fats/oils in treatment.
Iodine 2–4 g 24 hours Yellowish-brown staining, blue vomitus with starch, Iodine mumps, Ioderma, treat with Sodium Thiosulfate & starch.
