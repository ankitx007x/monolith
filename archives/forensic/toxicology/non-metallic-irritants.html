Non-Metallic Irritant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 555–558)

PHOSPHORUS

  • Exists in two allotropic forms:
    • White (Yellow) phosphorus
    • Red (Amorphous) phosphorus

White vs Red Phosphorus

Feature White Phosphorus Red Phosphorus
Colour White / Yellow Reddish-brown
Appearance Translucent, waxy cylinder Amorphous solid mass
Odour Garlicky Odourless
Taste Garlicky Tasteless
Luminosity Luminous in the dark Non-luminous
Exposure to air • Oxidizes rapidly
• Emits white fumes
• Ignites at 34°C
• Stored under water 		• Stable in air
• Does not fume
• Non-inflammable
Uses Fertilizers, rodenticides, vermin killers, fireworks, gunpowder Matchbox striking surface
Toxicity Highly toxic Practically non-toxic

Mechanism of Action

  • Acts as a protoplasmic poison.
  • Directly interferes with cellular oxidation.
  • Produces severe:
    • Hepatotoxicity
    • Nephrotoxicity
    • Cardiotoxicity
Mnemonic:
Protoplasmic Poison → Liver + Kidney + Heart

Clinical Features

1. Fulminant Poisoning

  • Occurs after ingestion of >1 g.
  • Death usually occurs within 12 hours.
  • Cause of death:
    • Shock
    • Cardiovascular collapse
  • If survival exceeds 12 hours:
    • Restlessness
    • Delirium
    • Maniacal behavior
  • Associated symptoms:
    • Intense thirst
    • Severe nausea
    • Persistent vomiting
    • Retching

2. Acute Poisoning

Three-stage clinical course

Stage I (Within hours to 3 days)
  • Burning pain in throat and abdomen.
  • Intense thirst.
  • Nausea and vomiting.
  • Diarrhea.
  • Abdominal pain.
  • Skin contact causes painful 2nd- and 3rd-degree burns.
  • Breath, vomitus and feces have a garlic odor.
  • Pathognomonic finding:
    • Luminescent ("smoking") vomitus
    • Luminescent ("smoking") stools
    • Smoky stool syndrome
Stage II
  • Latent symptom-free period.
  • Usually lasts 2–3 days.
Stage III
  • Multi-organ failure.
  • Systemic collapse.

GIT • Nausea

High-Yield Viva Points
  • Luminescent ("smoking") vomitus is pathognomonic of white phosphorus poisoning.
  • Garlic odor of breath, vomitus and stool is characteristic.
  • Death usually results from:
    • Shock
    • Acute hepatic failure
    • CNS damage

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 60–120 mg (White phosphorus)
  • Fatal Period: Usually within 24 hours (may be delayed to 5–7 days)

Treatment

  • Gastric lavage
    • 1:5000 potassium permanganate (KMnO4) solution.
    • Oxidizes phosphorus into harmless phosphoric acid/phosphates.
  • Activated charcoal
    • Adsorbs residual poison.
  • Copper sulphate lavage (0.2%)
    • Forms protective copper phosphide coating.
    • Use cautiously because of copper toxicity.
  • Vitamin K
    • 20 mg IV in repeated doses.
    • Corrects hypoprothrombinemia and bleeding.
  • Bowel evacuation
    • Magnesium sulphate purgation.
    • Repeat for several days if required.
  • Avoid oils and fats
    • Increase phosphorus absorption.
  • Supportive therapy
    • Glucose-saline infusion.
    • Plasma transfusion.
    • Noradrenaline for shock.
    • Correct fluid and electrolyte imbalance.
  • Renal support
    • Hemodialysis.
    • Peritoneal dialysis.
  • Dermal burns
    • Wash thoroughly with 1% copper sulphate solution.
Last-Minute Revision
  • Hallmark: Smoking (luminescent) vomitus & stool.
  • Odor: Garlic breath.
  • Mechanism: Protoplasmic poison.
  • Main organs: Liver → Kidney → Heart.
  • Fatal dose: 60–120 mg.
  • Avoid: Oils and fats.
  • Chemical antidote: KMnO4 lavage.

Post-Mortem Findings

External Findings

  • Body appears:
    • Emaciated
    • Jaundiced
    • Garlic odor present
  • Purpuric hemorrhages over the skin.
  • Corrosion of the oral mucous membrane.
  • Post-mortem hypostasis (lividity) is dark brown.

Internal Findings

  • Gastrointestinal tract
    • Mucosa yellow or grayish-white.
    • Softened and thickened.
    • Congested and inflamed.
    • Characteristic garlic odor.
    • Luminous phosphorus particles may be visible in the dark.
  • Hemorrhages
    • Multiple petechial hemorrhages involving:
      • Muscles
      • Serous membranes
      • Mucous membranes
      • Liver
      • Kidneys
      • Endocardium
      • Epicardium
      • Pericardium
      • Peritoneum
      • Lungs
      • Brain
  • Liver
    • Enlarged and swollen.
    • Yellow, soft and greasy.
    • Easily ruptured.
    • Acute yellow atrophy may develop in patients surviving more than 1 week.
  • Kidneys
    • Enlarged.
    • Yellow and greasy.
    • Hemorrhages over the mucosal surface.
  • Heart
    • Flabby.
    • Pale.
    • Marked fatty degeneration.
  • Lungs
    • Fat emboli present in pulmonary arterioles and capillaries.
  • Blood
    • Dark and tarry.
    • Poor coagulation.

Chronic Phosphorus Poisoning

  • Occurs due to prolonged inhalation of phosphorus fumes.
  • Common in workers exposed over several years in phosphorus industries.

Phossy Jaw (Glass Jaw)

  • Phosphorus fumes enter through:
    • Carious tooth
    • Alveolar interspace
  • Initial manifestation:
    • Persistent toothache
  • Progressive features:
    • Jaw swelling
    • Loose teeth
    • Gum necrosis
    • Sequestration of mandibular bone
  • Advanced disease:
    • Suppurative osteomyelitis
    • Necrosis of the mandible
    • Multiple discharging sinuses
    • Foul-smelling purulent discharge
High-Yield Viva:
Phossy Jaw = Chronic phosphorus exposure → Mandibular osteomyelitis + Bone necrosis + Discharging sinuses.

Systemic Features

  • GIT
    • Nausea
    • Vomiting
    • Anorexia
    • Abdominal pain
    • Indigestion
    • Purging
  • Musculoskeletal
    • Joint pain
    • Weight loss
  • Respiratory
    • Chronic bronchitis
  • Hematologic
    • Anemia
    • Bone marrow suppression
  • Hepatic
    • Jaundice

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Accidental poisoning
    • Children chewing fireworks.
    • Accidental ingestion of rat paste.
  • Homicidal poisoning
    • Mixed with alcohol or coffee to mask taste and odor.
    • May mimic acute liver disease.
  • Abortifacient
    • Taken orally.
    • Inserted vaginally to induce illegal abortion.
  • Military use
    • Grenade fragments.
    • Smoke screens.
    • Incendiary bullets.
  • Arson
    • White phosphorus wrapped in wet cloth or dung.
    • Thrown onto roofs.
    • Ignites spontaneously after drying.
Last-Minute Revision
  • Hallmark PM: Garlic odor + Luminous GI contents.
  • Liver: Fatty degeneration → Acute yellow atrophy.
  • Heart: Flabby with fatty degeneration.
  • Blood: Dark, tarry, poorly coagulable.
  • Chronic poisoning: Phossy jaw (Glass jaw).
  • Classical lesion: Mandibular osteomyelitis with sequestration.

IODINE

  • Bluish-black, soft, scaly crystals.
  • Metallic luster with an unpleasant taste.
  • Releases characteristic violet-colored vapors at all temperatures.

Mechanism of Action

  • Acts as a protoplasmic poison.
  • Fixes cellular proteins causing tissue necrosis.
  • Direct irritant to:
    • Respiratory tract
    • Skin
    • Mucous membranes
  • Binds bicarbonate ions leading to:
    • Severe metabolic acidosis
    • Acute renal failure
Mnemonic:
Protein Fixation → Tissue Necrosis → Metabolic Acidosis → Renal Failure

Clinical Features

Acute Poisoning

  • Inhalation
    • Upper respiratory tract irritation.
    • Glottic edema.
    • Pulmonary edema.
    • Pulmonary congestion.
  • Ingestion
    • Acts as an acid corrosive poison.
    • Burning pain from mouth to stomach.
    • Intense thirst.
    • Excessive salivation.
    • Vomiting and purging.
    • Vomitus and stools:
      • Dark yellow.
      • Blue if starch is present.
      • May contain blood.
      • Characteristic iodine odor.
    • Brown or yellowish-brown staining of lips and angles of the mouth.
    • Giddiness.
    • Limb cramps.
    • Fainting.
    • Weak pulse.
    • Cold, clammy skin.
  • Renal
    • Scanty red-brown urine.
    • Albuminuria.
    • Nephritis.
    • Acute renal failure.
  • Metabolic
    • Severe metabolic acidosis.
Tetrad of Iodine Poisoning
  • Red urine.
  • Iodine mumps (parotid swelling).
  • Ioderma (skin eruptions).
  • Yellow discoloration of skin and GI mucosa.

Chronic Poisoning (Iodism)

  • Catarrhal symptoms
    • Frontal sinus pain.
    • Rhinorrhea.
    • Conjunctivitis.
    • Bronchial catarrh.
    • Excessive salivation.
  • GIT
    • Nausea.
    • Vomiting.
    • Purging.
    • Emaciation.
  • Iodine mumps
    • Bilateral parotid gland enlargement.
  • Glandular atrophy
    • Breasts.
    • Testes.
    • Lymph nodes.
  • Ioderma
    • Acneiform eruptions.
    • Erythematous patches.
    • Urticaria.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 2–4 g.
  • Fatal Period: About 24 hours.

Treatment

  • Gastric lavage
    • Warm water containing soluble starch or albumen.
    • Forms insoluble blue starch-iodide complex.
  • Chemical antidote
    • 1–5% sodium thiosulfate solution.
    • Converts iodine into harmless iodide.
  • Promote excretion
    • Sodium chloride enhances urinary iodide excretion.
  • Demulcents
    • Arrowroot.
    • Barley water.
    • Alkalis.
  • Activated charcoal
    • Adsorbs free iodine.
  • Dermal exposure
    • Wash affected skin with 20% alcohol solution.
Last-Minute Revision
  • Mechanism: Protoplasmic poison.
  • Hallmark: Brown-stained lips + iodine odor.
  • Tetrad: Red urine, Iodine mumps, Ioderma, Yellow skin/GI mucosa.
  • Fatal dose: 2–4 g.
  • Chemical antidote: Sodium thiosulfate.
  • Lavage: Starch or albumen.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • External
    • Yellowish-brown staining of:
      • Skin
      • Lips
      • Oral mucosa
      • Clothing
  • Stomach
    • Gastric mucosa inflamed and excoriated.
    • Yellowish-brown staining.
    • Appears blue if starch is present in the stomach.
  • Body cavities
    • Characteristic iodine odor on opening the body.
  • Internal organs
    • Generalized congestion.
  • Heart, Liver & Kidneys
    • Fatty degeneration.
  • Brain
    • Cerebral edema.
