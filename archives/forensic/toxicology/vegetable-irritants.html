Vegetable Irritant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 559–565)

RICINUS COMMUNIS (CASTOR)

Active Principle

  • The entire plant is poisonous, containing toxalbumin ricin (a water-soluble glycoprotein) and a powerful allergen (CBA)[cite: 3].
  • Seeds contain the highest level of toxalbumin[cite: 3].
  • Seeds are rich in purgative oil[cite: 3].
  • Unbroken seeds are non-poisonous when swallowed or cooked[cite: 3].
  • Toxicity occurs when castor beans are thoroughly chewed or blenderized, even though the amount of ricin produced is small and poorly absorbed from the GIT[cite: 3].

Mechanism of Action

Ricin blocks protein synthesis through inhibition of RNA polymerase (belongs to a group of poisons known as A-B toxins)[cite: 3]. Ricin has a special binding protein that gains access to the endoplasmic reticulum in GIT mucosal cells, causing severe diarrhea[cite: 3].

Absorption and Excretion

  • Absorption: Through inhalation, ingestion, injection, and skin contact[cite: 3].
  • Excretion: By intestinal epithelium[cite: 3].

Clinical Features

Dust of Seeds May Cause

  • Watering of eyes and conjunctivitis[cite: 3]
  • Sneezing and acute nasal inflammation[cite: 3]
  • Headache and pharyngitis[cite: 3]
  • Asthmatic bronchitis and dermatitis[cite: 3]
  • Gastric upset[cite: 3]

On Ingestion (Seen within 10 h of Ingestion)

  • GIT: Burning pain in mouth, throat, and stomach; oral mucosa burning resembles alkali burn; salivation, severe abdominal pain, nausea, thirst, vomiting, bloody diarrhea[cite: 3].
  • CNS: Vertigo, drowsiness, delirium, convulsions[cite: 3].
  • Systemic: Cramps in calves and abdominal muscles; uremia, jaundice, rapid feeble pulse, cold clammy skin, dehydration[cite: 3].
  • Consciousness: Retained till death in some cases[cite: 3].

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 1–10 µg/kg body weight (by inhalation or injection)[cite: 3]. Oral exposure is far less toxic; lethal dose is about 2 mg/kg (10–20 seeds)[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Period: 3–5 days[cite: 3].

Treatment

  1. Gastric lavage[cite: 3]
  2. Activated charcoal[cite: 3]
  3. Demulcents[cite: 3]
  4. Administration of glucose and saline for dehydration[cite: 3]
  5. 2–5 g of sodium bicarbonate given 8 hourly by mouth to alkalinize urine[cite: 3]

Post-Mortem Findings

  • GIT mucosa congested, softened, and inflamed with occasional erosions and submucous hemorrhages (also produced on inhalation)[cite: 3].
  • Dilation of heart, petechial hemorrhages in pleura, edema, and congestion of liver, kidneys, spleen, and lungs[cite: 3].
  • Fragments of seeds may be found in stomach and intestines[cite: 3].
  • Hemorrhage in internal organs[cite: 3].

Medico-Legal Importance

  1. Accidental: Common in children eating seeds[cite: 3].
  2. Homicidal: Rare (powdered seeds used)[cite: 3].
  3. Powder of seeds causes conjunctivitis when applied to the eye[cite: 3].

ABRUS PRECATORIUS

Active Principles

All parts of the plant are poisonous[cite: 3]. Seed contains:

  • Abrin: A toxalbumin (similar to viperine snake poison)[cite: 3].
  • Abrine: N-methyltryptophan (amino acid) and hemagglutinin in cotyledons[cite: 3].
  • A lipolytic enzyme[cite: 3].
  • Abralin: A glycoside[cite: 3].

Clinical Features

On Ingestion

  • Symptoms delayed from a few hours up to 2–3 days[cite: 3].
  • Irritation of upper GIT, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, bloody diarrhea, rectal bleeding[cite: 3].
  • Weakness, cold perspiration, trembling of hands, weak rapid pulse, miosis[cite: 3].
  • Delayed cytotoxic effect in CNS, liver, kidney, and adrenal glands 2–5 days post-exposure[cite: 3].

On Injection under Animal Skin

  • Inflammation, edema, oozing of hemorrhagic fluid from site of puncture and necrosis[cite: 3].
  • Animal drops down, refuses feed, and dies in 3–4 days[cite: 3].
  • Tetanic convulsions occur or animal becomes cold, drowsy/comatose and dies[cite: 3].
  • Symptoms resemble viperine snakebite[cite: 3].

On Injection in Humans

  • Painful swelling and ecchymosis[cite: 3].
  • Inflammation and necrosis of muscle and regional lymph nodes[cite: 3].
  • Faintness, vertigo, vomiting, anorexia, fever, headache, dyspnea, prostration[cite: 3].
  • Convulsions before death from cardiac failure[cite: 3].

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 90–120 mg of abrin injected, or 1–2 crushed seeds orally (0.1–1 µg/kg body weight)[cite: 3]. Subcutaneous abrin is 100 times as toxic as oral route[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Period: 3–5 days[cite: 3].

Treatment

  1. Gastric lavage & activated charcoal[cite: 3]
  2. Purgatives[cite: 3]
  3. Injection of anti-abrin[cite: 3]
  4. Dissection and removal of the needle (Sui)[cite: 3]
  5. Sodium bicarbonate 10 g orally/day to maintain urinary alkalinity and prevent hemoglobinuria-induced renal blocking[cite: 3].

Post-Mortem Findings

  • Fragments of needle (Sui) may be found; edema at injection site[cite: 3].
  • Petechial hemorrhages under skin, pleura, pericardium, and peritoneum[cite: 3].
  • GIT hemorrhages, edema, and congestion[cite: 3].
  • Cerebral edema; congested liver, spleen, and kidneys[cite: 3].

Medico-Legal Importance

  1. Cattle Poisoning (SUIS): Leather workers use needles ("suis") made of Abrus precatorius decoted paste mixed with dhatura, opium, and onion to kill cattle for cheap hides or revenge[cite: 3]. Spikes are 15 mm long weighing 90–120 mg[cite: 3]. Driven into cattle flesh using a wooden handle or slapped between fingers for homicide[cite: 3].
  2. Malingerers use seed powder to produce conjunctivitis[cite: 3].
  3. Disturbs uterine function, used as abortifacient and arrow poison[cite: 3].

CALOTROPIS

Two main varieties: Calotropis gigantea (purple flowers) and Calotropis procera (white flowers)[cite: 3].

Active Principles

  • Uscharin[cite: 3]
  • Calotoxin[cite: 3]
  • Galactin[cite: 3]
  • Calotropin (glycoside)[cite: 3]
  • Trypsin (in milky juice of leaves and stem)[cite: 3]

Clinical Features

  • Local Application: Redness, formation of blisters which excoriate later[cite: 3].
  • Ingestion: Acrid bitter taste, burning pain in mouth/throat/abdomen, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dilated pupils, tetanic convulsions, circulatory collapse, death[cite: 3].

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: Uncertain[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Period: 6–12 hours[cite: 3].

Post-Mortem Findings & Medico-Legal Importance

  • Dilated pupils, froth at nostrils, stomatitis, acute GI inflammation, organ congestion[cite: 3].
  • Medicolegal: Used as abortifacient (applied via abortion stick), infanticide, cattle poison, and to produce artificial bruises[cite: 3]. Root of C. procera is toxic to cobras/snakes[cite: 3].

SEMECARPUS ANACARDIUM (MARKING NUT / BHILLAWA)

Black, heart-shaped nut with rough projections[cite: 3]. Pericarp contains brownish, oily, acrid juice that turns black on exposure to air[cite: 3].

Active Principles & Features

  • Active Principles: Semecarpol (0.1%), Bhilawanol (15–17%)[cite: 3].
  • Local Application: Irritation, painful blisters containing acrid serum, eczematous eruptions, itching; lesion resembles a bruise; later forms ulcer with sloughing[cite: 3].
  • Ingestion: Severe GIT irritation, blisters on throat, dyspnea, tachycardia, hypotension, cyanosis, loss of reflexes, delirium, coma, death[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Dose: 5–10 g (5–8 seeds)[cite: 3]. Fatal Period: 12–24 hours[cite: 3].

PLUMBAGO, COLOCYNTH & ERGOT

Plumbago (P. rosea, P. zeylanica)

  • Active Principle: Plumbagin (found in root)[cite: 3].
  • Features: Dermal application causes vesication/irritation; ingestion causes abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, shock, CNS depression[cite: 3].
  • Medicolegal: Root paste applied to cervical os for abortion; used to fabricate false bruises[cite: 3].

Colocynth (Citrullus colocynthis)

  • Active Principle: Colocynthin (glycoside in fruit, root, leaves)[cite: 3].
  • Features: Cholera-like clinical symptoms (watery diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, shock)[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Dose: 1–3 g[cite: 3]. Fatal Period: 24 hours[cite: 3].

Ergot (Claviceps purpurea)

  • Fungus infesting rye/barley grains[cite: 3]. Contains Ergotamine, Ergotoxin, Ergometrine, Dihydroergotamine[cite: 3].
  • Acute Features: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, paresthesia, muscle cramps, peripheral ischemia leading to gangrene of fingers/toes[cite: 3].
  • Chronic (Ergotism): Burning extremities, hemorrhagic vesication, pruritus, formication[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Dose: 1 to 3 g[cite: 3]. Fatal Period: 24 hours[cite: 3].

CARDIAC & NERVE POISONS

Aconite (Bikh / Monk's Hood)

  • Derived from Aconitum ferox root[cite: 3]. Active principle: Aconitine[cite: 3].
  • Mechanism: Inhibits repolarization causing repetitive firing of cardiac myocytes, opens Na+ channels, stimulates vagal medullary center[cite: 3].
  • Clinical Triad: Hypotension, Convulsions, Visual disturbances (hippus = alternate pupil constriction/dilatation)[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Dose: 1–2 g[cite: 3]. Fatal Period: 2–6 hours[cite: 3].
  • Medicolegal: Resembles horseradish root (Aconite cut section is pink with scars of rootlets; horseradish is white, cylindrical, and pungent)[cite: 3].

Hydrocyanic Acid (HCN / Cyanide)

  • Source: Bitter almond seeds, fruit seeds, burning plastic/cigarettes[cite: 3].
  • Fatal Dose: 200–300 mg KCN/NaCN; 50–100 mg HCN[cite: 3]. Fatal Period: 2–10 minutes[cite: 3].
  • Acute Triad: Cyanosis, convulsions, and tachycardia[cite: 3].
  • Antidotes: Amyl nitrite, sodium nitrite, sodium thiosulfate[cite: 3].
  • PM Finding: Cherry-red postmortem lividity, black stomach[cite: 3].

Memory Booster (High-Yield Triads)

  • Abrus precatorius: Hemorrhagic gastroenteritis + Viper bite-like injection signs + Demyelinating encephalitis[cite: 3].
  • Castor: Hemorrhage in organs/gastroenteritis + Dilated cardiomyopathy + Hemolysis with acute renal failure[cite: 3].
  • Croton: Increased bodily secretions + Vertigo + Circulatory shock[cite: 3].
  • Marking Nut: Blister formation + Hypotension + Delirium[cite: 3].
  • Plumbago: Vesication + Circulatory shock + CNS depression[cite: 3].
  • Cyanide: Cyanosis + Convulsions + Tachycardia[cite: 3].
