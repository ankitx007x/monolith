Vegetable Irritant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 559–565)

RICINUS COMMUNIS (CASTOR)

Active Principle

  • The entire plant is poisonous, containing toxalbumin ricin (a water-soluble glycoprotein) and a powerful allergen (CBA).
  • Seeds contain the highest level of toxalbumin.
  • Seeds are rich in purgative oil.
  • Unbroken seeds are non-poisonous when swallowed or cooked.
  • Toxicity occurs when castor beans are thoroughly chewed or blenderized, even though the amount of ricin produced is small and poorly absorbed from the GIT.

Mechanism of Action

Ricin blocks protein synthesis through inhibition of RNA polymerase (belongs to a group of poisons known as A-B toxins). Ricin has a special binding protein that gains access to the endoplasmic reticulum in GIT mucosal cells, causing severe diarrhea.

Absorption and Excretion

  • Absorption: Through inhalation, ingestion, injection, and skin contact.
  • Excretion: By intestinal epithelium.

Clinical Features

Dust of Seeds May Cause

  • Watering of eyes and conjunctivitis
  • Sneezing and acute nasal inflammation
  • Headache and pharyngitis
  • Asthmatic bronchitis and dermatitis
  • Gastric upset

On Ingestion (Seen within 10 h of Ingestion)

  • GIT: Burning pain in mouth, throat, and stomach; oral mucosa burning resembles alkali burn; salivation, severe abdominal pain, nausea, thirst, vomiting, bloody diarrhea.
  • CNS: Vertigo, drowsiness, delirium, convulsions.
  • Systemic: Cramps in calves and abdominal muscles; uremia, jaundice, rapid feeble pulse, cold clammy skin, dehydration.
  • Consciousness: Retained till death in some cases.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 1–10 µg/kg body weight (by inhalation or injection). Oral exposure is far less toxic; lethal dose is about 2 mg/kg (10–20 seeds).
  • Fatal Period: 3–5 days.

Treatment

  1. Gastric lavage
  2. Activated charcoal
  3. Demulcents
  4. Administration of glucose and saline for dehydration
  5. 2–5 g of sodium bicarbonate given 8 hourly by mouth to alkalinize urine

Post-Mortem Findings

  • GIT mucosa congested, softened, and inflamed with occasional erosions and submucous hemorrhages (also produced on inhalation).
  • Dilation of heart, petechial hemorrhages in pleura, edema, and congestion of liver, kidneys, spleen, and lungs.
  • Fragments of seeds may be found in stomach and intestines.
  • Hemorrhage in internal organs.

Medico-Legal Importance

  1. Accidental: Common in children eating seeds.
  2. Homicidal: Rare (powdered seeds used).
  3. Powder of seeds causes conjunctivitis when applied to the eye.

ABRUS PRECATORIUS

Active Principles

All parts of the plant are poisonous. Seed contains:

  • Abrin: A toxalbumin (similar to viperine snake poison).
  • Abrine: N-methyltryptophan (amino acid) and hemagglutinin in cotyledons.
  • A lipolytic enzyme.
  • Abralin: A glycoside.

Clinical Features

On Ingestion

  • Symptoms delayed from a few hours up to 2–3 days.
  • Irritation of upper GIT, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, bloody diarrhea, rectal bleeding.
  • Weakness, cold perspiration, trembling of hands, weak rapid pulse, miosis.
  • Delayed cytotoxic effect in CNS, liver, kidney, and adrenal glands 2–5 days post-exposure.

On Injection under Animal Skin

  • Inflammation, edema, oozing of hemorrhagic fluid from site of puncture and necrosis.
  • Animal drops down, refuses feed, and dies in 3–4 days.
  • Tetanic convulsions occur or animal becomes cold, drowsy/comatose and dies.
  • Symptoms resemble viperine snakebite.

On Injection in Humans

  • Painful swelling and ecchymosis.
  • Inflammation and necrosis of muscle and regional lymph nodes.
  • Faintness, vertigo, vomiting, anorexia, fever, headache, dyspnea, prostration.
  • Convulsions before death from cardiac failure.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 90–120 mg of abrin injected, or 1–2 crushed seeds orally (0.1–1 µg/kg body weight). Subcutaneous abrin is 100 times as toxic as oral route.
  • Fatal Period: 3–5 days.

Treatment

  1. Gastric lavage & activated charcoal
  2. Purgatives
  3. Injection of anti-abrin
  4. Dissection and removal of the needle (Sui)
  5. Sodium bicarbonate 10 g orally/day to maintain urinary alkalinity and prevent hemoglobinuria-induced renal blocking.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • Fragments of needle (Sui) may be found; edema at injection site.
  • Petechial hemorrhages under skin, pleura, pericardium, and peritoneum.
  • GIT hemorrhages, edema, and congestion.
  • Cerebral edema; congested liver, spleen, and kidneys.

Medico-Legal Importance

  1. Cattle Poisoning (SUIS): Leather workers use needles ("suis") made of Abrus precatorius decoted paste mixed with dhatura, opium, and onion to kill cattle for cheap hides or revenge. Spikes are 15 mm long weighing 90–120 mg. Driven into cattle flesh using a wooden handle or slapped between fingers for homicide.
  2. Malingerers use seed powder to produce conjunctivitis.
  3. Disturbs uterine function, used as abortifacient and arrow poison.

CALOTROPIS

Two main varieties: Calotropis gigantea (purple flowers) and Calotropis procera (white flowers).

Active Principles

  • Uscharin
  • Calotoxin
  • Galactin
  • Calotropin (glycoside)
  • Trypsin (in milky juice of leaves and stem)

Clinical Features

  • Local Application: Redness, formation of blisters which excoriate later.
  • Ingestion: Acrid bitter taste, burning pain in mouth/throat/abdomen, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dilated pupils, tetanic convulsions, circulatory collapse, death.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: Uncertain.
  • Fatal Period: 6–12 hours.

Post-Mortem Findings & Medico-Legal Importance

  • Dilated pupils, froth at nostrils, stomatitis, acute GI inflammation, organ congestion.
  • Medicolegal: Used as abortifacient (applied via abortion stick), infanticide, cattle poison, and to produce artificial bruises. Root of C. procera is toxic to cobras/snakes.

SEMECARPUS ANACARDIUM (MARKING NUT / BHILLAWA)

Black, heart-shaped nut with rough projections. Pericarp contains brownish, oily, acrid juice that turns black on exposure to air.

Active Principles & Features

  • Active Principles: Semecarpol (0.1%), Bhilawanol (15–17%).
  • Local Application: Irritation, painful blisters containing acrid serum, eczematous eruptions, itching; lesion resembles a bruise; later forms ulcer with sloughing.
  • Ingestion: Severe GIT irritation, blisters on throat, dyspnea, tachycardia, hypotension, cyanosis, loss of reflexes, delirium, coma, death.
  • Fatal Dose: 5–10 g (5–8 seeds). Fatal Period: 12–24 hours.

PLUMBAGO, COLOCYNTH & ERGOT

Plumbago (P. rosea, P. zeylanica)

  • Active Principle: Plumbagin (found in root).
  • Features: Dermal application causes vesication/irritation; ingestion causes abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, shock, CNS depression.
  • Medicolegal: Root paste applied to cervical os for abortion; used to fabricate false bruises.

Colocynth (Citrullus colocynthis)

  • Active Principle: Colocynthin (glycoside in fruit, root, leaves).
  • Features: Cholera-like clinical symptoms (watery diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, shock).
  • Fatal Dose: 1–3 g. Fatal Period: 24 hours.

Ergot (Claviceps purpurea)

  • Fungus infesting rye/barley grains. Contains Ergotamine, Ergotoxin, Ergometrine, Dihydroergotamine.
  • Acute Features: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, paresthesia, muscle cramps, peripheral ischemia leading to gangrene of fingers/toes.
  • Chronic (Ergotism): Burning extremities, hemorrhagic vesication, pruritus, formication.
  • Fatal Dose: 1 to 3 g. Fatal Period: 24 hours.

CARDIAC & NERVE POISONS

Aconite (Bikh / Monk's Hood)

  • Derived from Aconitum ferox root. Active principle: Aconitine.
  • Mechanism: Inhibits repolarization causing repetitive firing of cardiac myocytes, opens Na+ channels, stimulates vagal medullary center.
  • Clinical Triad: Hypotension, Convulsions, Visual disturbances (hippus = alternate pupil constriction/dilatation).
  • Fatal Dose: 1–2 g. Fatal Period: 2–6 hours.
  • Medicolegal: Resembles horseradish root (Aconite cut section is pink with scars of rootlets; horseradish is white, cylindrical, and pungent).

Hydrocyanic Acid (HCN / Cyanide)

  • Source: Bitter almond seeds, fruit seeds, burning plastic/cigarettes.
  • Fatal Dose: 200–300 mg KCN/NaCN; 50–100 mg HCN. Fatal Period: 2–10 minutes.
  • Acute Triad: Cyanosis, convulsions, and tachycardia.
  • Antidotes: Amyl nitrite, sodium nitrite, sodium thiosulfate.
  • PM Finding: Cherry-red postmortem lividity, black stomach.

Memory Booster (High-Yield Triads)

  • Abrus precatorius: Hemorrhagic gastroenteritis + Viper bite-like injection signs + Demyelinating encephalitis.
  • Castor: Hemorrhage in organs/gastroenteritis + Dilated cardiomyopathy + Hemolysis with acute renal failure.
  • Croton: Increased bodily secretions + Vertigo + Circulatory shock.
  • Marking Nut: Blister formation + Hypotension + Delirium.
  • Plumbago: Vesication + Circulatory shock + CNS depression.
  • Cyanide: Cyanosis + Convulsions + Tachycardia.
