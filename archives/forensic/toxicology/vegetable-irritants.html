Vegetable Irritant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 559–565)

RICINUS COMMUNIS (CASTOR)

Active Principle

  • Poisonous principle: Ricin (toxalbumin; water-soluble glycoprotein) and a potent allergen (CBA).
  • Entire plant is poisonous, but seeds contain the highest concentration of ricin.
  • Seeds are rich in purgative castor oil.
  • Intact or cooked seeds are usually non-poisonous because ricin is not released.
  • Poisoning occurs when seeds are chewed, crushed, or blenderized, releasing ricin.
  • Oral absorption is relatively poor, but toxicity can still be severe.

Mechanism of Action

  • Ricin is an A-B toxin.
  • Inhibits protein synthesis by inactivating ribosomes (often described as inhibition of RNA-dependent protein synthesis).
  • Enters GI mucosal cells via receptor-mediated binding.
  • Causes:
    • Massive mucosal injury
    • Severe secretory diarrhea
    • Cell death and multi-organ toxicity

Absorption & Excretion

Absorption

  • Ingestion
  • Inhalation
  • Injection
  • Skin contact (rare)

Excretion

  • Mainly through the intestinal epithelium.

Clinical Features

Dust Exposure

  • Watering of eyes
  • Conjunctivitis
  • Sneezing
  • Acute rhinitis
  • Headache
  • Pharyngitis
  • Asthmatic bronchitis
  • Dermatitis
  • Gastric upset

Ingestion

Symptoms usually appear within 10 hours.

System Features
GIT • Burning pain in mouth, throat & stomach
• Oral mucosal burns resembling alkali burns
• Excessive salivation
• Severe abdominal pain
• Nausea & intense thirst
• Vomiting
• Bloody diarrhea
CNS • Vertigo
• Drowsiness
• Delirium
• Convulsions
Systemic • Painful calf and abdominal muscle cramps
• Dehydration
• Cold clammy skin
• Rapid feeble pulse
• Jaundice
• Uremia
Consciousness May remain conscious until death.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose
    • Inhalation/Injection: 1–10 µg/kg
    • Oral: Approximately 2 mg/kg (about 10–20 chewed seeds)
  • Fatal Period: 3–5 days.

Treatment

  • Gastric lavage (if appropriate).
  • Activated charcoal.
  • Demulcents.
  • Correct dehydration with glucose-saline.
  • Sodium bicarbonate 2–5 g orally every 8 hours to alkalinize urine.
  • Aggressive supportive and symptomatic management.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • GIT:
    • Congested, softened, inflamed mucosa
    • Erosions and submucosal hemorrhages
  • Heart: Dilatation.
  • Pleura: Petechial hemorrhages.
  • Liver, kidneys, spleen & lungs:
    • Congestion
    • Edema
  • Fragments of castor seeds may be present in the stomach and intestines.
  • Hemorrhages may be present in multiple internal organs.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Accidental: Most common, especially in children eating castor seeds.
  • Homicidal: Rare; powdered seeds may be administered.
  • Powdered seeds applied to the eye can produce severe conjunctivitis.
High-Yield Points
  • Active toxin: Ricin (toxalbumin).
  • Most poisonous part: Seeds.
  • Whole seeds are usually harmless; chewed/crushed seeds are toxic.
  • Mechanism: Inhibits protein synthesis (A-B toxin).
  • Characteristic presentation: Severe hemorrhagic gastroenteritis with dehydration.
  • Fatal period: 3–5 days.

ABRUS PRECATORIUS (Rosary Pea / Jequirity)

Active Principles

  • All parts of the plant are poisonous.
  • Seeds contain:
    • Abrin – toxalbumin (resembles viper venom in action).
    • Abrine – N-methyltryptophan with hemagglutinin in the cotyledons.
    • Lipolytic enzyme.
    • Abralin – glycoside.

Mechanism of Action

  • Abrin is an A-B toxalbumin.
  • Inhibits protein synthesis, resulting in cell death.
  • Produces severe local tissue necrosis and systemic multi-organ toxicity.
  • Highly toxic by injection; oral toxicity occurs only when seeds are crushed or chewed.

Clinical Features

Ingestion

Symptoms appear after a few hours to 2–3 days.

System Features
GIT • Upper GI irritation
• Abdominal pain
• Nausea & vomiting
• Bloody diarrhea
• Rectal bleeding
General • Weakness
• Cold perspiration
• Tremors of hands
• Weak, rapid pulse
• Miosis
Delayed Toxicity • CNS injury
• Hepatic damage
• Renal damage
• Adrenal involvement (2–5 days after exposure)

Injection in Animals

  • Inflammation and edema at injection site.
  • Hemorrhagic discharge from puncture wound.
  • Local tissue necrosis.
  • Animal refuses food and collapses.
  • Tetanic convulsions or coma.
  • Death usually occurs within 3–4 days.
  • Clinical picture resembles viper snakebite.

Injection in Humans

  • Painful swelling with ecchymosis.
  • Inflammation and necrosis of muscles.
  • Regional lymphadenitis.
  • Vertigo and faintness.
  • Vomiting and anorexia.
  • Headache and fever.
  • Dyspnea and marked prostration.
  • Convulsions preceding death.
  • Death usually results from cardiac failure.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose
    • Injection: 90–120 mg abrin
    • Oral: 1–2 crushed seeds (approximately 0.1–1 µg/kg)
  • Subcutaneous abrin is approximately 100 times more toxic than oral exposure.
  • Fatal Period: 3–5 days.

Treatment

  • Gastric lavage.
  • Activated charcoal.
  • Purgatives.
  • Anti-abrin serum (where available).
  • Surgical exploration and removal of the poisoned needle (Sui), if injected.
  • Sodium bicarbonate 10 g/day orally to maintain alkaline urine and reduce hemoglobinuric renal damage.
  • Supportive care with fluids and symptomatic treatment.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • Poisoned needle (Sui) may be found at the injection site.
  • Local edema and tissue necrosis around puncture wound.
  • Petechial hemorrhages involving:
    • Skin
    • Pleura
    • Pericardium
    • Peritoneum
  • GIT:
    • Congestion
    • Edema
    • Hemorrhages
  • Brain: Cerebral edema.
  • Liver, spleen & kidneys: Congested.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Cattle poisoning (Sui):
    • Leather workers prepare poisoned needles (Suis) from Abrus paste mixed with dhatura, opium, and onion.
    • Used to kill cattle for valuable hides or as revenge.
    • Typical Sui:
      • Length: ~15 mm
      • Weight: 90–120 mg
    • May also be used for homicide by inserting into human tissues.
  • Malingerers may apply seed powder to the eye to produce conjunctivitis.
  • Used as an abortifacient.
  • Used as an arrow poison.
High-Yield Points
  • Active toxin: Abrin (toxalbumin).
  • Mechanism: Inhibits protein synthesis (A-B toxin).
  • Injection is about 100× more toxic than oral ingestion.
  • Characteristic medico-legal weapon: Sui.
  • Resembles viper snakebite when injected.
  • Fatal period: 3–5 days.

CALOTROPIS

  • Varieties:
    • Calotropis gigantea – Purple flowers
    • Calotropis procera – White flowers

Active Principles

  • Uscharin
  • Calotoxin
  • Galactin
  • Calotropin (cardiac glycoside)
  • Trypsin (present in the milky latex of leaves and stem)

Clinical Features

Local Application

  • Redness and irritation.
  • Painful blisters.
  • Excoriation after rupture of blisters.
  • Dermatitis resembling chemical burns.

Ingestion

  • Acrid, bitter taste.
  • Burning pain in mouth, throat, and abdomen.
  • Nausea and vomiting.
  • Diarrhea.
  • Dilated pupils.
  • Tetanic convulsions.
  • Circulatory collapse.
  • Death in severe poisoning.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: Not established.
  • Fatal Period: 6–12 hours.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • Dilated pupils.
  • Froth around nostrils.
  • Stomatitis.
  • Acute gastroenteritis.
  • Congestion of internal organs.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Used as an abortifacient (applied on abortion sticks).
  • Used for infanticide.
  • Used as a cattle poison.
  • Milky latex may be applied to produce artificial bruises.
  • Root of Calotropis procera is poisonous to cobras and other snakes.
High-Yield Points
  • Characteristic latex: Milky white.
  • Produces severe blistering dermatitis.
  • Common medico-legal use: Artificial bruises & abortifacient.
  • Fatal period: 6–12 hours.

SEMECARPUS ANACARDIUM (MARKING NUT / BHILLAWA)

  • Black, heart-shaped nut with rough surface projections.
  • Pericarp contains a brown, oily, acrid juice.
  • Juice turns black on exposure to air.

Active Principles

  • Semecarpol (≈0.1%).
  • Bhilawanol (15–17%).

Clinical Features

Local Application

  • Severe irritation.
  • Painful blisters filled with acrid serum.
  • Eczematous eruptions.
  • Intense itching.
  • Lesion initially resembles a bruise.
  • Later ulceration with slough formation.

Ingestion

  • Severe gastroenteritis.
  • Blister formation in mouth and throat.
  • Dyspnea.
  • Tachycardia.
  • Hypotension.
  • Cyanosis.
  • Loss of reflexes.
  • Delirium.
  • Coma.
  • Death.

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 5–10 g (approximately 5–8 seeds).
  • Fatal Period: 12–24 hours.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Frequently used to produce artificial bruises.
  • Sometimes used by malingerers to fabricate injuries.
  • Occasionally used as an abortifacient.

Distinction: Contusion vs Marking Nut Lesion

Feature Contusion Marking Nut Lesion
Shape Usually regular Irregular
Margins Diffuse Sharp and well-defined
Colour changes Present with time Absent
Itching Absent Marked
Extravasation of blood Present Absent
Blisters Absent Present
Nail marks Usually absent Scratch marks common due to itching
Cause Mechanical rupture of capillaries Chemical injury to skin
High-Yield Points
  • Active toxins: Semecarpol & Bhilawanol.
  • Characteristic lesion: Artificial bruise with blistering and itching.
  • Presence of blisters and absence of colour changes help differentiate from true contusions.
  • Fatal period: 12–24 hours.

PLUMBAGO, COLOCYNTH & ERGOT

Plumbago (P. rosea, P. zeylanica)

  • Active Principle: Plumbagin (found in root).
  • Features: Dermal application causes vesication/irritation; ingestion causes abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea, shock, CNS depression.
  • Medicolegal: Root paste applied to cervical os for abortion; used to fabricate false bruises.

Colocynth (Citrullus colocynthis)

  • Active Principle: Colocynthin (glycoside in fruit, root, leaves).
  • Features: Cholera-like clinical symptoms (watery diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, shock).
  • Fatal Dose: 1–3 g. Fatal Period: 24 hours.

Ergot (Claviceps purpurea)

  • Fungus infesting rye/barley grains. Contains Ergotamine, Ergotoxin, Ergometrine, Dihydroergotamine.
  • Acute Features: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, paresthesia, muscle cramps, peripheral ischemia leading to gangrene of fingers/toes.
  • Chronic (Ergotism): Burning extremities, hemorrhagic vesication, pruritus, formication.
  • Fatal Dose: 1 to 3 g. Fatal Period: 24 hours.

CARDIAC & NERVE POISONS

Aconite (Bikh / Monk's Hood)

Source & Active Principle

  • Obtained from the root of Aconitum ferox.
  • Active principle: Aconitine.

Mechanism of Action

  • Opens persistent sodium (Na+) channels causing prolonged depolarization.
  • Inhibits repolarization, producing repetitive firing of cardiac myocytes.
  • Stimulates the vagal medullary center causing marked bradycardia and hypotension.

Clinical Features

  • Cardiovascular: Hypotension, bradycardia, arrhythmias.
  • CNS: Convulsions.
  • Eye: Visual disturbances with hippus (alternate constriction and dilatation of pupils).
Clinical Triad:
  • Hypotension
  • Convulsions
  • Visual disturbances (Hippus)

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Fatal Dose: 1–2 g.
  • Fatal Period: 2–6 hours.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Root resembles horseradish and may cause accidental poisoning.
  • Aconite root: Pink cut surface with scars of rootlets.
  • Horseradish root: White, cylindrical and pungent.

Hydrocyanic Acid (HCN / Cyanide)

Sources

  • Bitter almond seeds.
  • Fruit kernels.
  • Burning plastics.
  • Cigarette smoke.

Clinical Features

Acute Cyanide Triad
  • Cyanosis
  • Convulsions
  • Tachycardia

Fatal Dose & Fatal Period

  • Potassium/Sodium Cyanide: 200–300 mg.
  • Hydrocyanic Acid: 50–100 mg.
  • Fatal Period: 2–10 minutes.

Antidotes

  • Amyl nitrite.
  • Sodium nitrite.
  • Sodium thiosulfate.

Post-Mortem Findings

  • Cherry-red postmortem lividity.
  • Black discoloration of the stomach.

High-Yield Triads
  • Abrus precatorius: Hemorrhagic gastroenteritis + Viper bite-like injection signs + Demyelinating encephalitis.
  • Castor: Hemorrhage in organs + Gastroenteritis + Dilated cardiomyopathy with hemolysis causing acute renal failure.
  • Croton: Increased bodily secretions + Vertigo + Circulatory shock.
  • Marking Nut: Blister formation + Hypotension + Delirium.
  • Plumbago: Vesication + Circulatory shock + CNS depression.
  • Cyanide: Cyanosis + Convulsions + Tachycardia.
