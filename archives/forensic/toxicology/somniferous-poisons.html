Forensic Medicine: Somniferous Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 590–593)

1. Opium & Opiate Derivatives

Classification: Neurotoxic (Cerebral) — Somniferous Poison.

Opium is the dried juice obtained from the unripe poppy plant capsule (Papaver somniferum).

Derivatives of Opium

  • Crude Opium (natural): Morphine, Codeine.
  • Semi-synthetic Opium: Heroin, Hydromorphone, Oxymorphone, Oxycodone.
  • Synthetic Opium: Methadone, Meperidine, Fentanyl.

Chemical Groups / Active Principles

  • Phenanthrene Group (Narcotic): Morphine, Codeine, Thebaine.
  • Isoquinoline Group (Analgesic, non-narcotic): Papaverine, Noscapine.

Mechanism of Action & Receptors

Three major classes of opiate receptors are present:

  • Mu (μ) Receptor:
    • μ1: Supraspinal analgesia, peripheral analgesia, euphoria.
    • μ2: Spinal analgesia, respiratory depression, physical dependence, GI dysmotility, miosis.
    • Most clinically used opiates are selective for the μ receptor.
  • Kappa (κ) Receptor: Subtypes κ1, κ2, κ3 (Analgesia at the spinal cord level).
  • Delta (δ) Receptor
Pharmacokinetics & Lethal Parameters:
• Metabolism: Conjugation with glucuronic acid to form morphine-6-glucuronide; excreted in urine as morphine-3-glucuronide.
• Duration of Action: Pethidine (2 hours) | Morphine, Codeine, Heroin (4 hours) | Methadone (8 hours).
• Fatal Dose: Opium: 2 g | Morphine: 0.2 g | Codeine: 0.5 g | Methadone: 100 mg.
• Fatal Period: 6 to 12 hours.

2. Clinical Features & Management of Opium Poisoning

Stages of Acute Poisoning

  1. Stage of Excitement: Increased sense of well-being, increased mental activity, freedom from anxiety, talkativeness, restlessness, hallucinations, flushing of face, mania.
  2. Stage of Stupor: Headache, nausea, vomiting, incapacity for exertion, sense of weight in limbs, giddiness, drowsiness progressing to stupor and coma; contracted pupils, cyanosed face/lips, generalized itching sensation; normal pulse and respiration initially.
  3. Stage of Coma (Narcosis): Flaccid muscles, abolished reflexes, pale face, congested conjunctiva, pinpoint pupils, suspended secretions (except sweat), cold skin, subnormal temperature, slow full pulse, low BP, Cheyne-Stokes respiration. Death occurs in deep coma.
Pathognomonic Triad in Acute Opioid Poisoning (@CPR):
1. Coma
2. Pinpoint pupils (dilated if severe terminal hypoxia)
3. Respiratory depression (bradypnea, cyanosis, pulmonary edema)

Diagnostic Test: Marquis Test: 3 mL conc. H2SO4 + 3 drops formalin added to suspected residue → Purple-red color changing to violet and then blue.

Treatment of Acute Opioid Poisoning

  • Maintenance of airway; intubation if required.
  • Gastric lavage (indicated even if route was IV, IM, SC, or rectal).
  • Suspension of activated charcoal introduced into stomach.
  • Enema twice daily for two days.
  • Specific Antidote — Naloxone: Adult: 1.2 mg/kg; Child: 0.4 mg/kg repeated doses (competitively blocks opioid receptors).
  • Manage convulsions with benzodiazepines; general symptomatic support.
Intoxication vs. Withdrawal Differential:
• Constricted Pupils: Indicates active Opium Intoxication.
• Dilated Pupils: Indicates Opium Withdrawal.
• Other Withdrawal Features: Yawning, dysphoric mood, increased water loss from all orifices (lacrimation, vomiting, rhinorrhea, sweating, diarrhea), and elevated vitals.

Chronic Poisoning (Morphinism / Morphinomania)

Addicts can tolerate 3 to 6 g per day. Features include emaciation, dry skin, injection scars/abscesses, GI disturbances (anorexia, constipation, furred tongue), irritability, fatigue, impotence/frigidity, moral degradation, and mental depression.

Treatment: Methadone 30–40 mg daily (tapered off), Dihydrocodeine/Codeine, Propranolol 80 mg (for anxiety/craving), tranquilizers at bedtime, and psychiatric counseling.

3. Heroin, Codeine & Body Packer Syndromes

Heroin (Diacetylmorphine)

  • Forms: White, Brown, Black tar. Speedballs = Heroin + Cocaine.
  • Most dangerous drug of addiction; fatal dose is 50 mg.
  • Metabolized to 6-monoacetylmorphine (6-MAM) or acetylmorphine.
  • Produces intense euphoria for several minutes followed by ~1 hour sedation; total effect lost in 3–6 hours.
  • Treatment: Methadone (40 mg daily prevents withdrawal), detoxification, and antagonists (Naltrexone, Naloxone, Haloperidol, Clonidine, Cyclazocine).

Codeine (Methylmorphine)

  • Effective orally due to lower first-pass hepatic metabolism.
  • Analgesic action relies on conversion to morphine. Lethal level: 0.5 mg/100 mL.
  • Illicitly used to synthesize morphine/heroin via demethylation.

Body Packer vs. Body Stuffer Syndrome

Feature Body Packer (Surgical Mule) Syndrome Body Stuffer Syndrome
Definition Smuggling illicit drugs compressed into packages (approx. 25 × 12 mm) wrapped in latex/condoms/foil. Hastily swallowing/inserting illegal drugs to conceal evidence from authorities.
Purpose Organized trafficking / transport across borders. Emergency concealment during police interception.
Complication Lethal toxicity if package ruptures; intestinal obstruction. Acute toxicity due to poorly sealed, rapid absorption.
Diagnosis & Management X-ray, USG, CT scan; laxatives or surgical removal. Diazepam 10 mg IV (repeated for sedation), fluids, IV glucose/thiamine, oxygen.

4. Postmortem Findings & Medico-Legal Aspects

Autopsy Findings in Opioid Poisoning

  • External Findings: Skin ulcers, pseudoaneurysms, needle track marks (antecubital fossa, forearms, hands, neck, groin, ankles), tattooing hiding needle marks, "skin popping" scars, emaciation. Cyanotic face and nails, white/pinkish froth at mouth and nostrils. Characteristic smell of opium.
  • Internal Findings: Opium lumps in stomach, congested trachea/bronchi covered with froth, severely edematous and congested lungs, cerebral/meningeal congestion, pulmonary hemorrhage, SABE. Deep tissue myositis, thrombophlebitis, lymphangitis, osteomyelitis.
  • Viscera Collection: Liver, brain, stomach + contents, lymph nodes, blood (test for HIV, HBsAg, 6-MAM), urine, and bile.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Among the most commonly abused illicit substances worldwide.
  • Morphine and pethidine are frequently abused by medical personnel.
  • Accidental death ("death on the needle") is extremely common among IV drug users.
  • Suicidal use occurs due to painless death; homicidal use is rare due to bitter taste and strong odor.
  • Rarely used as cattle poison, for horse doping, or to steady nerves prior to committing crimes.
Barbiturate Poisoning Features (Quick Differential):
• CNS depression: Drowsiness → Stupor → Coma, loss of reflexes, positive Babinski sign.
• Skin: Formation of characteristic bullae and blisters.
• CVS Collapse: Cyanosis, cold clammy skin, deep yellow concentrated urine, hypotension.
• Pupils: Constricted initially (reacting to light), dilating during terminal asphyxia.

5. Memory Booster (Quick Summary)

Key Principles:
• Source: Dried latex of Papaver somniferum. Seeds are non-poisonous (used as condiments).
• Active Principles: Phenanthrenes (Morphine, Codeine) = Narcotic; Isoquinolines (Papaverine) = Non-narcotic.
• Classic Triad: Coma + Pinpoint Pupils + Respiratory Depression.
• Fatal Dose: Morphine (200 mg) | Pethidine (1 g) | Heroin (50 mg).
• Antidote of Choice: Naloxone / Nalmefene.
