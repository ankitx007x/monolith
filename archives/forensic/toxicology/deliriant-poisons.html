Forensic Medicine: Deliriant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 594–598)

1. Dhatura Fastuosa

Classification: Neurotoxic (Cerebral) — Deliriant Poison. Deliriants act on the brain to induce altered consciousness with confusion, delusions, hallucinations, and agitation.

Two main varieties exist: Dhatura alba and Dhatura niger. All parts of the plant, including nectar, are poisonous.

Active Principles & Mechanism of Action

  • Active Principles: Hyoscine (scopolamine), Hyoscyamine, and traces of Atropine.
  • Mechanism: Competitively inhibits the muscarinic effects of acetylcholine at postganglionic parasympathetic sites and postganglionic sympathetic innervation of sweat glands and smooth muscle.
  • CNS Action: Initial stimulation of higher cerebral centers followed by depression and paralysis of vital medullary centers. Respiration is initially stimulated, then depressed. High doses (>10 mg) induce profound delirium.
Dose-Related Atropinic Progression:
• 0.5 mg: Dryness of mouth.
• 1.0 mg: Blurring of vision.
• 2.0 mg: CVS effects (tachycardia, elevated RR, hypertension).
• 5.0 mg: Hyperpyrexia, urinary retention.
• 10.0 mg: Delirium.

Lethal Parameters: Fatal Dose: 0.6 to 1 gram (or ~100–125 seeds) | Fatal Period: 24 hours.

Clinical Presentation (The 10 D's of Dhatura)

Presentation ranges from delirium to coma:

  1. Dryness of mouth & throat
  2. Difficulty in talking
  3. Dysphagia
  4. Dilatation of cutaneous blood vessels
  5. Dilatation of pupils (Mydriasis)
  6. Dry hot skin
  7. Dysuria & urine retention
  8. Drunken gait (Ataxia)
  9. Delirium
  10. Drowsiness leading to coma
Classical Diagnostic Rhyme:
• Dry as a bone (Inhibition of salivary & sweat secretions)
• Red as a beet (Flushing/dilatation of cutaneous vessels)
• Blind as a bat (Cycloplegia & severe pupillary dilatation)
• Hot as a hare (Hyperpyrexia due to lack of sweating)
• Mad as a wet hen (Agitation, delirium, amnesia, hallucinations)

Note: Pollen contact can cause unilateral mydriasis known as "cornpicker's pupil".

Differential Diagnosis: Dhatura vs. Capsicum Seeds

Feature Dhatura Seeds Capsicum Seeds
Size & Thickness Large and thick Small and thin
Shape Kidney-shaped Rounded
Color Dark or yellowish-brown Pale-yellow
Margin Laterally compressed, double-edged at convex border Convex border is simple and sharp
Surface Numerous small depressions Smooth
Smell & Taste Odourless; Bitter taste Pungent smell; Pungent taste
Embryo (Longitudinal Section) Curved outward at the hilum Curved inward

Management & Autopsy Findings

  • Management: Emetics or gastric lavage using a weak solution of tannic acid; administer activated charcoal and cathartics; wash lower bowel frequently.
  • Specific Antidote: Physostigmine (1 mg IV or IM hourly). Pilocarpine nitrate (5 mg SC) helps peripheral symptoms but does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
  • Contraindications: Avoid Morphine due to added risk of respiratory center depression. Control delirium with short-acting barbiturates or bromides.
  • Autopsy Findings: Non-specific; seeds resist putrefaction for long periods and may be identified in stomach contents. Mild gastric mucosal inflammation, pulmonary edema, and general asphyxial signs.
  • Medico-Legal Aspects: Commonly used as a "Railroad Poison". Powdered seeds or extracts are mixed with food/drinks offered to travelers to stupefy them prior to robbery, rape, or kidnapping. Accidental poisoning occurs in children eating fruit. A person acting under Dhatura delirium is not criminally responsible.

2. Cannabis Sativa / Indica (Hemp)

Active Principle: Cannabinoids, primarily Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol (Δ9-THC). Acts on CB1 receptors (brain — deliriant effects) and CB2 receptors (reticuloendothelial system).

Common Preparations

  • Bhang: Dried leaves and stems made into a decoction (oral). Least potent.
  • Ganja: Resinous extract of leaves and flowering/fruiting tops of the female plant (smoked).
  • Charas / Hashish: Pure dried resin collected from flowering tops (smoked). Most potent.
  • Marijuana: Crushed dried flowering tops smoked in cigarettes or pipes.
  • Majun: Sweetmeat prepared with plant extracts.
Lethal Dose Parameters (@BCG):
• Bhang: 10 g/kg
• Charas: 2 g/kg
• Ganja: 8 g/kg
• Pure THC: 30 mg/kg
• Fatal Period: Several days / ~12 hours.

Clinical Presentation & Complications

  • Psychiatric Symptoms (@6DAS): Detachment, disinhibition, depersonalization, euphoria, feeling of cleverness, dreaminess, paranoia, altered sense of time/space, vivid hallucinations, speech fragmentation, chest tightness, fear of death.
  • Physical Symptoms: Increased appetite/thirst, dry mouth, dizziness, ataxia, tremors, tachycardia, urinary frequency, somnolence progressing to deep sleep. Patient wakes after ~6 hours with exhaustion and impaired cognition.
  • Chronic Toxicity:
    • Amotivational Syndrome: Anorexia, weight loss, apathy, disinclination to work, moral deterioration.
    • Running Amok (Hashish Insanity): Psychic disturbance characterized by a depression phase followed by a violent, frenzied homicidal spree against anyone in the path, ending in suicide or surrender.

Treatment & Medico-Legal Importance

  • Treatment: Gastric lavage, activated charcoal, cathartics. Support with IV 50% glucose (100 mL), thiamine (100 mg), and naloxone (2 mg). Control severe agitation with Diazepam (5–10 mg IV) or Haloperidol. Reassurance and psychotherapy.
  • Medico-Legal Aspects: Commonly abused drug. Majun and Charas are used by road poisoners to stupefy victims. Sometimes taken by criminals to strengthen nerves before executing crimes. Used by ascetics/fakirs to overcome hunger, thirst, and worldly attachments. Nabilone is a synthetic cannabinoid used as an antiemetic in cancer chemotherapy.

3. Cocaine

Obtained from leaves of Erythroxylum coca. Forms include Cocaine Hydrochloride (white soluble powder) and Freebase / Crack (transparent crystalline base that crackles when heated).

Pharmacodynamics & Active Principles

  • Active Alkaloids: Ecgonine, hygrine, cinnamyl cocaine. Final metabolite is ecgonine.
  • Mechanism: Blocks reuptake of catecholamines (dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine). Increased dopamine causes intense CNS stimulation/delirium; increased NE/Epi causes sympathomimetic effects (tachycardia, severe hypertension, sweating) resembling pheochromocytoma.
  • Local Action: Potent local anesthetic (desensitizes nerve endings) and local vasoconstrictor.
Lethal Dose Parameters:
• Fatal Dose: 1 gram orally (or 50 mg pure alkaloid).
• Fatal Period: Few minutes to a few hours.
• Speedball: Combination of Cocaine adulterated with Heroin.

Clinical Stages & Chronic Effects

  • Stage of Euphoria: Hypervigilance, grandiosity, talkativeness, tachycardia, arrhythmias, dilated pupils (mydriasis), sweating, chills, tactile hallucinations.
  • Stage of Depression: Loss of reflexes, coma, paralysis, circulatory and respiratory collapse, sudden cardiac death.
  • Chronic Abuse (Cocainism / Cocainomania): Anorexia, dark/black tongue and teeth, shifty gaze, sunken eyes, emaciation, loss of libido, gynecomastia.
    • Magnan's Symptom (Formication / Cocaine Bugs): Hallucination where the patient feels tiny insects or snakes crawling under or over the skin.
    • Complications: Nasal septum ulceration/perforation from chronic snorting, persistent rhinitis, cocaine psychosis, mesenteric ischemia, excited delirium, and acute myocardial infarction.

Management & Postmortem Findings

  • Treatment: Decontaminate site (gastric lavage if oral; rinse nasal mucosa with water if snorted; apply tourniquet/ligature if injected). Control seizures with short-acting barbiturates or chloroform. Support vitals, administer IV Thiamine and Naloxone. Specific antidote: Amyl Nitrite.
  • Autopsy Findings: Severe asphyxial signs, pulmonary congestion and edema, cerebral hemorrhage, focal myocardial scarring, infective endocarditis (due to unsterile IV injection), hepatic necrosis (in coca paste smokers), and nasal septum perforation.
Important Board / Viva Notes:
1. Liquid Gold: Slang for urine of amphetamine addicts, sold on the street because ~40% of amphetamine is excreted unchanged.
2. Urine pH Manipulation: Urine is alkalinized in Barbiturate and Salicylate poisoning; urine is acidified in Morphine, Strychnine, and Amphetamine poisoning.
3. Body Packer vs. Stuffer: Cocaine is frequently involved in both Body Packer (smuggling via sealed packets) and Body Stuffer (emergency swallowing to destroy evidence) syndromes.

4. Memory Booster (Quick Summary)

Deliriant Poisons Summary (@ABCD):
• Atropa belladonna / Amph-Cocaine
• Bhang (Cannabis sativa)
• Cocaine
• Datura fastuosa

Key Features:
• Dhatura: Anticholinergic toxidrome (10 D's); Antidote: Physostigmine; Classic railroad poison.
• Cannabis: Act on CB1/CB2; Amotivational syndrome & Running Amok; Active constituent: Δ9-THC.
• Cocaine: Blocks dopamine/NE reuptake; 3 M's (Mydriasis, Magnan's bugs, Mesenteric ischemia); Antidote: Amyl nitrite.
