Forensic Medicine: Deliriant Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 594–598)

1. Dhatura Fastuosa

Classification

  • Neurotoxic (Cerebral) Poison
  • Classified as a Deliriant Poison.
  • Produces:
    • Delirium
    • Hallucinations
    • Confusion
    • Agitation
    • Altered consciousness
  • Main varieties:
    • Dhatura alba
    • Dhatura niger
  • All parts of the plant are poisonous, including the nectar.

Active Principles

  • Hyoscine (Scopolamine) ⭐
  • Hyoscyamine
  • Traces of Atropine

Mechanism of Action

  • Competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist.
  • Blocks the action of acetylcholine at:
    • Postganglionic parasympathetic nerve endings.
    • Sympathetic cholinergic fibers supplying sweat glands.
  • CNS effects:
    • Initial cerebral stimulation.
    • Followed by CNS depression.
    • Paralysis of vital medullary centres in severe poisoning.
  • Respiration:
    • Initially stimulated.
    • Later depressed.
  • >10 mg atropine equivalent → Severe delirium.

Dose-Related Atropinic Effects

Dose Clinical Effect
0.5 mg Dry mouth
1 mg Blurred vision
2 mg Tachycardia, ↑ BP, ↑ Respiratory rate
5 mg Hyperpyrexia, Urinary retention
10 mg Delirium

High-Yield Facts

  • Fatal Dose: 0.6–1 g
  • Equivalent: 100–125 seeds
  • Fatal Period: ~24 hours

Clinical Features (The 10 D's of Dhatura)

  1. Dry mouth & throat
  2. Difficulty in talking
  3. Dysphagia
  4. Dilatation of cutaneous blood vessels (Flushing)
  5. Dilated pupils (Mydriasis)
  6. Dry, hot skin
  7. Dysuria with urinary retention
  8. Drunken gait (Ataxia)
  9. Delirium
  10. Drowsiness → Coma

Classical Anticholinergic Rhyme ⭐

  • Dry as a bone
    • Dry mouth and absence of sweating.
  • Red as a beet
    • Cutaneous flushing.
  • Blind as a bat
    • Mydriasis + Cycloplegia.
  • Hot as a hare
    • Hyperpyrexia.
  • Mad as a wet hen
    • Hallucinations, delirium, agitation and amnesia.
Special Viva Point
  • Pollen entering one eye may cause unilateral mydriasis.
  • This is called "Cornpicker's Pupil."

Dhatura Seeds vs Capsicum Seeds

Feature Dhatura Capsicum
Size Large & thick Small & thin
Shape Kidney-shaped Rounded
Colour Dark / Yellowish-brown Pale yellow
Margin Double-edged Simple sharp margin
Surface Pitted (small depressions) Smooth
Taste Bitter Pungent
Odour Odourless Pungent
Embryo Curved outward Curved inward

Treatment

  • Emesis or gastric lavage with weak tannic acid solution.
  • Activated charcoal.
  • Cathartics.
  • Frequent bowel wash if required.
  • Specific antidote → Physostigmine
    • 1 mg IV/IM
    • May be repeated hourly if required.
  • Pilocarpine nitrate
    • 5 mg SC.
    • Improves peripheral symptoms.
    • Does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
  • Control delirium with:
    • Short-acting barbiturates
    • Bromides
  • Avoid Morphine (may worsen respiratory depression).

Autopsy Findings

  • Generally non-specific.
  • Dhatura seeds may be recovered from stomach contents (resist putrefaction).
  • Mild gastritis.
  • Pulmonary edema.
  • General asphyxial signs.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • "Railroad Poison" ⭐
    • Mixed with food or drinks.
    • Victim becomes delirious and helpless.
    • Used for robbery, rape and kidnapping.
  • Accidental poisoning:
    • Children eating the fruits.
  • A person committing an act during Dhatura delirium is generally not criminally responsible.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls ⭐

  • Poison type → Deliriant (Anticholinergic)
  • Active principles → Hyoscine • Hyoscyamine • Atropine
  • Mechanism → Muscarinic receptor blockade
  • Fatal dose → 0.6–1 g (100–125 seeds)
  • Fatal period → 24 hours
  • 10 D's of Dhatura ⭐
  • "Dry as a bone, Red as a beet, Blind as a bat, Hot as a hare, Mad as a wet hen" ⭐
  • Cornpicker's pupil → Unilateral mydriasis
  • Specific antidote → Physostigmine
  • Railroad poison ⭐

2. Cannabis Sativa / Indica (Hemp)

Active Principle

  • Δ9-Tetrahydrocannabinol (Δ9-THC) ⭐
  • Acts on:
    • CB1 receptors → Brain (psychoactive effects)
    • CB2 receptors → Reticuloendothelial/Immune system

Common Preparations

Preparation Source Use Potency
Bhang Dried leaves & stems Oral (drink/decoction) Least potent ⭐
Ganja Flowering tops of female plant Smoked Moderate
Charas (Hashish) Pure dried resin Smoked Most potent ⭐
Marijuana Dried flowering tops Smoked -
Majun Sweet preparation Oral -

High-Yield Facts

  • Bhang: 10 g/kg
  • Ganja: 8 g/kg
  • Charas: 2 g/kg
  • Pure THC: 30 mg/kg
  • Fatal period: About 12 hours to several days

Clinical Features

Psychiatric Features (@6DAS)

  • Detachment
  • Disinhibition
  • Depersonalization
  • Euphoria
  • Feeling of cleverness
  • Dreaminess
  • Paranoia
  • Altered perception of time & space
  • Visual and auditory hallucinations
  • Fragmented speech
  • Chest tightness
  • Fear of death

Physical Features

  • Dry mouth
  • Increased appetite ("Munchies")
  • Excessive thirst
  • Dizziness
  • Ataxia
  • Tremors
  • Tachycardia
  • Urinary frequency
  • Somnolence → Deep sleep
  • Wakes after ~6 hours with exhaustion and impaired concentration.

Chronic Toxicity

  • Amotivational Syndrome
    • Anorexia
    • Weight loss
    • Apathy
    • Lack of motivation
    • Moral deterioration
  • Running Amok (Hashish Insanity)
    • Initial depression.
    • Followed by sudden violent homicidal behavior.
    • Ends with suicide or surrender.

Treatment

  • Gastric lavage.
  • Activated charcoal.
  • Cathartics.
  • Supportive care.
  • Coma cocktail
    • 50% Glucose — 100 mL
    • Thiamine — 100 mg
    • Naloxone — 2 mg
  • Diazepam (5–10 mg IV) for agitation.
  • Haloperidol if psychosis is severe.
  • Reassurance and psychotherapy.

Medico-Legal Importance

  • Common drug of abuse.
  • Majun and Charas used by road poisoners.
  • Sometimes taken before committing crimes to reduce fear.
  • Used by ascetics/fakirs to suppress hunger and thirst.
  • Nabilone is a synthetic cannabinoid used as an antiemetic in chemotherapy.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls ⭐

  • Active constituent → Δ9-THC
  • Receptors → CB1 (Brain), CB2 (Immune system)
  • Least potent → Bhang
  • Most potent → Charas (Hashish)
  • Chronic feature → Amotivational syndrome
  • Hashish insanity → Running Amok

3. Cocaine

Source

  • Obtained from the leaves of Erythroxylum coca.
  • Forms:
    • Cocaine hydrochloride (white powder)
    • Freebase / Crack cocaine (crystalline form)

Active Alkaloids

  • Ecgonine ⭐
  • Hygrine
  • Cinnamyl cocaine
  • Final metabolite → Ecgonine

Mechanism of Action

  • Blocks reuptake of:
    • Dopamine
    • Norepinephrine
    • Epinephrine
  • Produces:
    • Powerful CNS stimulation.
    • Sympathomimetic effects.
  • Acts as:
    • Local anesthetic.
    • Local vasoconstrictor.

High-Yield Facts

  • Fatal dose: 1 g orally (≈50 mg pure alkaloid)
  • Fatal period: Minutes to a few hours
  • Speedball = Cocaine + Heroin ⭐

Clinical Stages

Stage of Euphoria

  • Hypervigilance
  • Grandiosity
  • Talkativeness
  • Tachycardia
  • Arrhythmias
  • Mydriasis
  • Sweating
  • Chills
  • Tactile hallucinations

Stage of Depression

  • Loss of reflexes
  • Coma
  • Paralysis
  • Respiratory failure
  • Circulatory collapse
  • Sudden cardiac death

Chronic Toxicity (Cocainism)

  • Anorexia
  • Dark/black tongue and teeth
  • Shifty gaze
  • Sunken eyes
  • Emaciation
  • Loss of libido
  • Gynecomastia

Important Complications

  • Magnan's Symptom (Formication)
    • Feeling of insects/snakes crawling under the skin.
    • Also called "Cocaine Bugs."
  • Nasal septum ulceration/perforation.
  • Persistent rhinitis.
  • Cocaine psychosis.
  • Mesenteric ischemia.
  • Excited delirium.
  • Acute myocardial infarction.

Treatment

  • Remove unabsorbed drug:
    • Gastric lavage (oral ingestion)
    • Wash nasal mucosa (snorting)
    • Tourniquet if injected
  • Support airway, breathing and circulation.
  • Control seizures with short-acting barbiturates.
  • IV Thiamine + Naloxone.
  • Specific antidote (as per your notes): Amyl Nitrite.

Autopsy Findings

  • Marked asphyxial signs.
  • Pulmonary edema and congestion.
  • Cerebral hemorrhage.
  • Myocardial scarring.
  • Infective endocarditis (IV users).
  • Hepatic necrosis.
  • Nasal septum perforation.

Last-Minute Viva Pearls ⭐

  • Mechanism → Blocks dopamine & norepinephrine reuptake
  • Local anesthetic + Vasoconstrictor
  • Speedball = Cocaine + Heroin
  • Magnan's symptom = Cocaine bugs (Formication)
  • Mydriasis ⭐
  • Mesenteric ischemia ⭐
  • Nasal septum perforation ⭐

Extra Viva Facts

  • Liquid Gold → Urine of amphetamine addicts.
  • Urine alkalinization → Barbiturate & Salicylate poisoning.
  • Urine acidification → Morphine, Strychnine & Amphetamine poisoning.
  • Body packer → Swallows well-sealed drug packets for smuggling.
  • Body stuffer → Quickly swallows poorly wrapped packets to avoid arrest.
