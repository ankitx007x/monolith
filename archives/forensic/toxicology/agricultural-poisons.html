Agricultural Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 601–604)

Classification

  • Insecticides: Organophosphorous, Carbamates, Organochlorines
  • Rodenticides: Phosphorous, Aluminium Phosphide, Anticoagulants, Strychnine
  • Herbicides: Paraquat and Diquat
  • Fungicides
  • Nematicides
  • Acaricides: Mites, ticks, spiders (Kelthane), Pyrethrins and Pyrethroids, Naphthalene
Note: Most common form of suicide in many regions is by insecticide ingestion.

ORGANOPHOSPHOROUS POISONS

Classification of OP Compounds

  • Alkyl Phosphates: HETP, TEPP, OMPA, Malathion
  • Aryl Phosphates: Parathion, Paraoxon, Methyl-parathion, Chlorthion, Diazinon
Exam Note: In the classification of organophosphorus poisons, all those having suffix -ion and -on are aryl phosphates except malathion, demeton, and trichlorfon.

Absorption & Mechanism of Action

Absorption: Through transdermal, transconjunctival, inhalational, ingestion, and direct injection routes.

Organophosphorous compound
↓
Inhibits acetylcholinesterase in all parts of the body
↓
Acetylcholine accumulates at parasympathetic, sympathetic, and somatic sites
↓
Continuous stimulation of local receptors
↓
Eventual paralysis of nerve or muscle

Clinical Features

1. Muscarinic Manifestations

Acts on smooth muscles, heart, and exocrine glands.

  • Bronchial tree: Bronchoconstriction, increased bronchial secretion, dyspnea, pulmonary edema, cyanosis.
  • GIT: Anorexia, salivation, nausea, vomiting, cramps, diarrhea, tenesmus.
  • Sweat glands: Increased sweating.
  • Salivary glands: Increased salivation.
  • Lacrimal glands: Increased lacrimation (tears may be pink due to accumulation of porphyrin — chromodacryorrhea).
  • CVS: Arrhythmias, conduction blocks, hypotension.
  • Pupils: Miosis (occasionally unequal or dilated).
  • Ciliary body: Blurred vision.
  • Bladder: Urinary incontinence.
Acronym: SLUDGE
• Salivation
• Lacrimation (chromodacryorrhea)
• Urination
• Defecation
• Gastrointestinal distress
• Emesis

2. Nicotinic Manifestations

Acts on skeletal muscle and autonomic ganglia.

  • Skeletal muscle: Initially contraction/fasciculations, later paralysis.
  • Sympathetic ganglia: Hypertension, tachycardia, pallor, mydriasis.

3. CNS Manifestations

  • Restlessness, emotional lability, headache, tremors.
  • Anxiety, drowsiness, confusion, slurred speech.
  • Generalized weakness, convulsions, coma.
  • Depression of cardiovascular and respiratory centers.

Chronic Poisoning

Occurs as an occupational hazard among agricultural workers.

Diagnosis

  • Estimation of cholinesterase activity: RBC cholinesterase and Plasma cholinesterase.
  • P-Nitrophenol test.
  • Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC).
  • Diagnostic Atropine Challenge: Confirmed by giving 2 mg IV atropine, which relieves muscarinic symptoms without causing signs of atropinization in poisoned patients.

Treatment

A. Prehospital Treatment

  • Decontamination and supportive care, including airway, breathing, and respiration.

B. Hospital Care

  1. Gastric Lavage: With oral administration of activated charcoal if patient presents within 60 minutes of ingestion.
  2. Atropine (Specific Antidote): Counteracts muscarinic effects.
    • Given as 3–5 ampoules (1.8–3 mg) in adults and 0.05–0.1 mg/kg in children below 12 years bolus IV every 5–20 minutes until atropinization is attained.
    • Signs of atropinization: Mid-dilated pupils, HR >80 bpm, Systolic BP >80 mmHg, clear air entry on auscultation, dry axilla & tongue, flushing of skin.
    • Once atropinized, administer 20% of the total loading dose every hour via IV infusion in normal saline for 24–48 hours, tapering gradually.
  3. Pralidoxime / PAM (Specific Antidote): Reactivates phosphorylated acetylcholinesterase and works on nicotinic effects.
    • Administer 2 g in 100 mL NS (30 mg/kg) over 30 minutes, followed by an infusion of 1 g every 4 hours.
  4. Supportive Measures: Furosemide for pulmonary congestion, benzodiazepines for seizure control, oxygen support, ICU monitoring.
Contraindicated Drugs: Morphine, aminophylline, phenothiazine, and reserpine should NEVER be given in OP poisoning.

Postmortem Findings

External Findings

  • Kerosene-like smell (since OP compounds are formulated in kerosene solvents).
  • Signs of asphyxia: Face congested, cyanosis of lips, fingers, and nose.
  • Blood-stained froth at mouth and nose.
  • Constricted pupils.

Internal Findings

  • Stomach contents smell of kerosene; gastric mucosa congested with submucous petechial hemorrhages.
  • Lungs show gross congestion, excessive edema, and subpleural petechiae.
  • Brain, meninges, and internal organs are hyperemic and congested; cerebral edema present.
  • Microscopic exam of heart: Dilatation of pericardial blood vessels, interstitial edema, inflammatory cells, hemosiderin-laden macrophages, and toxic myocarditis with fatty infiltration.
  • Putrefaction is delayed.

CARBAMATES

Derivatives of carbonic acid.

  • Extremely / Highly Toxic (LD50: 1–500 mg/kg): Carbaryl, carbofuran, methomyl, propoxur.
  • Moderately / Slightly Toxic (LD50: >500 mg/kg): Aldicarb, carbendazim, triallate.

Action & Clinical Features

  • Binds to acetylcholinesterase at esteric and anionic sites. This bond is weak and reversible.
  • Produces muscarinic and nicotinic symptoms identical to organophosphates.
  • Difference from OP: Spontaneously hydrolyzes from cholinesterase within 24 to 48 hours; does NOT cross the blood-brain barrier effectively.

Treatment

  • Atropine: Main treatment to reduce muscarinic effects.
  • Oximes are CONTRAINDICATED: Carbamate-AChE bond is weak, anionic site is not free for oxime binding, and oximes themselves have weak anticholinesterase activity, worsening toxicity.

ALUMINIUM PHOSPHIDE (Al Phosphide)

Available in tablet form (e.g., Celphos, QuickPhos). Each 3g tablet liberates 1g of phosphine gas. Possesses a characteristic garlicky / rotten fish odor.

Uses: Pesticide, insecticide, grain preservative, rodenticide.

Fatal Dose: 0.5 g (1 tablet). Fatal Period: 1 to 4 hours.

Action

In contact with water/moisture or gastric HCl, aluminium phosphide releases phosphine gas:

AlP + 3H2O → Al(OH)3 + PH3 ↑

Phosphine gas inhibits cytochrome oxidase, blocking cellular respiration and causing widespread cellular hypoxia and organ damage.

Clinical Features

  • Mild cases: Nausea, vomiting, headache, abdominal pain.
  • Moderate to Severe cases:
    • GIT: Retrosternal pain, severe epigastric burning, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea.
    • CVS: Refractory hypotension, cardiogenic shock, myocarditis, arrhythmias, heart failure. (Cardiogenic shock is the most common cause of death).
    • Respiratory: Cough, dyspnea, cyanosis, ARDS, pulmonary edema.
    • Hepatic & Renal: Jaundice, hepatitis, acute renal failure.
    • CNS: Headache, dizziness, altered mental status, delirium.

Diagnosis & Chemical Tests

  • Garlicky odor on breath or gastric contents.
  • Silver Nitrate Test (Biological Sample Test): Gastric aspirate or tissue in steam distillation flask + HCl/H2SO4 heated to 50°C for 15 min. Distillate passed into 1% AgNO3 solution turns black if phosphine is present.

Treatment

  • Gastric Lavage: Using 1:1000 Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4) or Coconut oil / Liquid paraffin.
  • Activated Charcoal: Given orally to adsorb phosphine.
  • No Specific Antidote: Treatment is supportive and symptomatic.
  • Management of shock (IV fluids, Inotropes like dopamine/norepinephrine).
  • Magnesium Sulfate Therapy (3g IV bolus followed by 6g infusion over 24 hrs for 5–7 days) to stabilize cardiac membranes.
  • Sodium Bicarbonate for severe metabolic acidosis; H2-blockers/PPIs for stomach pain.

Postmortem Findings

  • Pungent garlic/rotten fish odor upon opening body cavities.
  • Stomach mucosa shows mucosal shedding, intense congestion, and hemorrhagic spots.
  • Liver & Kidneys: Necrotic changes and cloudy swelling.
  • Heart: Toxic myocarditis with fibrillar necrosis.
  • Lungs: ARDS features, congestion, pulmonary edema.

Medicolegal Importance

Mostly suicidal; occasionally accidental in agricultural workers or grain handlers.

PARAQUAT AND DIQUAT

Bipyridyl compounds used widely as non-selective herbicides. Granular or water-soluble dark brown liquids.

Fatal Dose: 3 to 5 g (or 10–15 mL of 20% solution). Fatal Period: 2 to 7 days.

Action

Undergoes nicotinamide-dependent reduction to form free radicals. These react with molecular oxygen to produce superoxide and hydroxyl radicals, causing cell membrane destruction, lipid peroxidation, and multi-organ cell death.

Clinical Features

  • Local / GIT: Severe burning, oropharyngeal ulceration, tongue corrosion, vomiting, hematemesis.
  • Renal: Oliguric or non-oliguric renal failure.
  • Lungs: Progressive pulmonary fibrosis, cough, hemoptysis, pulmonary edema, severe dyspnea.
  • Hepatic & CVS: Centrilobular necrosis, cholestasis, coma, convulsions, cerebral edema.

Treatment

  • Gastric lavage within 1 hour.
  • Administration of 1 liter of 15–30% suspension of Fuller's Earth or Activated Charcoal to bind herbicide.
  • Hemodialysis and hemoperfusion within 12 hours.
  • Note: Supplemental high-flow oxygen should be avoided in early stages as oxygen accelerates free radical formation and lung damage.
