Forensic Medicine: Spinal Poisons

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 599–600)

1. Strychnos Nux Vomica (Strychnine)

Classification

  • Neurotoxic (Spinal) Poison ⭐

Source

  • Obtained from the seeds of Strychnos nux-vomica.
  • Seeds are extremely bitter.

Active Principles

  • Strychnine → Present in seeds; powerful spinal convulsant ⭐
  • Brucine → Present in bark
  • Loganine

Uses

  • Rodenticide (Rat poison).
  • Used to kill stray dogs ("Dog Buttons").

Mechanism of Action

  • Competitive blockade of glycine receptors in the spinal cord and brainstem.
  • Removes inhibitory action of glycine → exaggerated reflex activity.
  • Produces:
    • Generalized tonic convulsions
    • Spastic muscle paralysis
  • GABA transmission remains unaffected.

High-Yield Viva Facts ⭐

  • Fatal dose: 50–100 mg (1–2 crushed seeds)
  • Fatal period: 1–2 hours
  • Main cause of death: Respiratory failure

2. Clinical Features

Early Symptoms

  • Anxiety
  • Nausea
  • Mydriasis
  • Muscle twitching
  • Progresses rapidly to generalized tonic convulsions.

Classic Features (@ROPE) ⭐

  • R → Risus Sardonicus (grimacing smile)
  • O → Opisthotonus (backward arching)
  • P → Pleurothotonus (lateral bending)
  • E → Emprosthotonus (forward bending)
  • Generalized convulsions.
  • Complete relaxation between convulsions. ⭐
  • Trismus (lockjaw).
  • Mydriasis.
  • Consciousness remains intact until death. ⭐

Complications

  • Hyperthermia
  • Rhabdomyolysis
  • Myoglobinuria
  • Acute renal failure
  • Respiratory failure (most common cause of death)

3. Treatment

Control Convulsions

  • Diazepam IV (first-line).
  • Phenobarbital.
  • If uncontrolled:
    • General anesthesia.
    • Neuromuscular blockers:
      • Succinylcholine
      • Curare
      • Gallamine
      • Pancuronium

Supportive Care

  • Keep patient in a dark, quiet room. ⭐
  • Avoid noise, light and touch because even minor stimuli trigger convulsions.

Specific Antidote (As per notes)

  • Short-acting barbiturates:
    • Phenobarbital
    • Sodium Amytal

Decontamination

  • Gastric lavage with warm water + dilute KMnO4.
  • Activated charcoal.
  • Acidify urine to enhance excretion. ⭐

4. Strychnine vs Tetanus

Feature Strychnine Poisoning Tetanus
History Poison ingestion History of wound/trauma
Onset Sudden Gradual
Fever Usually absent initially Present
Convulsions Whole body simultaneously Sequential muscle involvement
Trismus Occurs with generalized convulsions Usually earliest feature
Between Fits Complete relaxation ⭐ Persistent rigidity ⭐
Fatal Period 1–2 hours >24 hours
Laboratory Strychnine detected chemically Clostridium tetani isolated

5. Postmortem Findings

  • Postmortem caloricity (temperature rises instead of falling). ⭐
  • Early onset and early disappearance of rigor mortis.
  • General asphyxial signs.
  • Congestion of gastrointestinal tract.
  • Serosal hemorrhages.
  • Muscle tears/hemorrhages (occasionally).
  • Congested spinal cord.
  • Histology:
    • Capillary ring hemorrhages in anterior & posterior horns.
    • Neuronal chromatolysis.

Viscera Preservation ⭐

  • Routine viscera.
  • Entire spinal cord.
  • One-half of brain.
  • Whole heart.

6. Medico-Legal Importance

  • Homicidal poisoning possible using crushed seeds or alkaloid.
  • Suicidal poisoning is rare because death is painful.
  • Accidental poisoning:
    • Medicinal overdose
    • Accidental substitution
    • Children eating seeds
  • Also used as:
    • Arrow poison
    • Cattle poison
    • Aphrodisiac (illicit use)

Last-Minute Viva Pearls ⭐

  • Source → Strychnos nux-vomica seeds
  • MOA → Glycine receptor blockade
  • GABA unaffected
  • Fatal dose → 50–100 mg (1–2 seeds)
  • Fatal period → 1–2 hours
  • Main cause of death → Respiratory failure
  • Consciousness preserved until death
  • Complete relaxation between convulsions
  • Dark quiet room is essential
  • Urine should be acidified
