Asphyxiants

FAST TRACK CLINICAL SCIENCE MBBS - 3rd YEAR (pp. 612–615)

Classification of Noxious Gases

Henderson and Haggard Classification:

  1. Irritants: Smoke, tear gases, ammonia, formaldehyde, chlorine, phosgene, sulfur dioxide.
  2. Chemical Asphyxiants: Carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulfide, arsine, carbon disulfide, and cyanide.
  3. Simple Asphyxiants: Carbon dioxide, methane, helium, nitrogen, and nitrous oxide.
  4. Volatile Drugs: Aliphatic hydrocarbons, halogenated hydrocarbons, aromatic hydrocarbons.
  5. Systemic Poisons: Insecticides, arsine, stibine.

CARBON MONOXIDE (CO)

Properties & Sources

  • Properties: Colorless, odorless, tasteless, non-irritating gas, lighter than air, insoluble in water, burns with a blue flame.
  • Sources: Incomplete combustion of organic matter/fuels, tobacco smoke, automobile exhaust (1–7% CO), faulty heating units, coal gas, fires, endogenous production.

Mechanism of Action

  • Combines reversibly with hemoglobin to form carboxyhemoglobin (COHb), producing anemic hypoxia. Has ~250 times higher affinity for Hb than oxygen.
  • Blocks cellular respiration by inhibiting cytochrome a3 oxidase and cytochrome P450.
  • Combines with myoglobin (~15% of body CO; affinity ~40 times higher than O2), causing direct myocardial depression.
  • Shifts oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve to the left, decreasing O2 delivery to peripheral tissues.

Signs and Symptoms by COHb Level

COHb (%) Clinical Features
0–10% No symptoms.
10–20% Breathlessness, mild headache, abdominal pain.
20–30% Throbbing headache, irritability, emotional instability, buzzing in ears.
30–40% Severe headache, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, dimness of vision, confusion, ataxia.
40–50% Increasing confusion, hallucinations, rapid respiration, staggering and incoordination (mistaken for drunkenness).
50–70% Weak thready pulse, hypotension, irregular respiration, convulsions, coma, and death.
> 80% Rapid death from respiratory arrest.

Characteristic Clinical Findings

  • Skin Bullae: Erythematous blisters over pressure points (calves, buttocks, wrists, knees) due to skin hypoxia, resembling 2nd-degree burns.
  • CO Automatism: Victim shows erratic swinging movements in attempt to escape, disturbing furniture/clothes before succumbing.
  • Ocular: Flame-shaped retinal hemorrhages and bright red retinal veins.

Treatment

  • Remove victim from exposure to fresh air immediately.
  • 100% normobaric oxygen via tight-fitting reservoir mask or ET tube.
  • Hyperbaric Oxygen (HBO): At 2–3 atmospheres PO2 with 5% CO2. Reduces COHb half-life dramatically.
  • Mannitol (20% IV) for cerebral edema.
  • IV fluids and inotropes for hypotension and shock.

Postmortem Findings

  • Cherry-red Coloration: Characteristic cherry-red stain on skin, mucous membranes, blood, postmortem lividity, and internal viscera (associated with COHb >30%).
  • Brain: Bilateral necrosis of basal ganglia and globus pallidus (most characteristic in delayed deaths).
  • Heart & Lungs: Subepicardial/petechial hemorrhages, myocardial necrosis, pulmonary edema.
  • Blood: Pinkish-red and fluid.

CARBON DIOXIDE (CO2)

Properties & Sources

  • Heavy, colorless, odorless (slightly pungent) gas with an acidic taste. Normal atmospheric content ~0.04%.
  • Solid form is known as dry ice. Found in deep wells, mine shafts, sewers, and fermentation vats.

Mechanism & Symptoms

  • Combines with hemoglobin to form carbaminohemoglobin and dissolves in plasma forming carbonic acid, leading to severe respiratory acidosis and asphyxia.
  • Symptoms: 2–5% causes hyperpnea and throbbing headache; 5–10% causes mental confusion and muscular tremors; >20% leads to rapid unconsciousness, convulsions, coma, and asphyxial death.

Treatment & PM Findings

  • Shift to fresh air, artificial respiration with 100% O2, supportive care.
  • PM Findings: Classic features of asphyxia — marked dark cyanosis, fluid dark blood, capillary congestion, and petechial hemorrhages.

HYDROGEN SULFIDE (H2S)

Properties & Sources

  • Colorless gas with a strong characteristic odor of rotten eggs.
  • Found in sewers (sewer gas), manure pits, petroleum refineries, tanneries, and decaying organic matter.

Action & Symptoms

  • Combines with methemoglobin to form sulfmethemoglobin. Inhibits cytochrome oxidase system, causing cellular asphyxiation.
  • High concentrations (>200 ppm) cause rapid olfactory fatigue (loss of smell); >700–1000 ppm causes immediate cardiopulmonary arrest ("knockdown").

Treatment & PM Findings

  • 100% Oxygen. Antidote: Amyl Nitrite inhalation or Sodium Nitrite IV (induces methemoglobinemia to bind H2S).
  • PM Findings: Rotten egg odor around nostrils; greenish discoloration of internal organs, brain gray matter, and liver due to sulfmethemoglobin formation.

HYDROGEN CYANIDE (HCN) & CYANIDES

Properties & Sources

  • Gas or liquid with a distinct odor of bitter almonds. Hydrocyanic acid solution is known as Prussic acid.
  • Found in kernel of fruits (almonds, apples, apricots, cherries) as amygdalin. Used in electroplating, photography, fumigation, and chemical synthesis.

Mechanism of Action

Protoplasmic cytotoxic poison causing histotoxic anoxia. Inactivates cytochrome oxidase (Fe3+), arresting cellular ATP production.

Clinical Features

  • Onset is rapid (seconds to minutes).
  • Triad of Diagnostic Findings:
    1. Narrow arterial-venous oxygen difference (venous blood remains bright red).
    2. Anion gap metabolic acidosis.
    3. Elevated blood lactate concentration (>10 mmol/L).
  • Breath smells of bitter almonds. Skin appears bright pink or brick-red.
  • Convulsions, opisthotonus, dilated pupils, cardiovascular collapse, coma, and respiratory arrest.

Fatal Dose & Period

  • Fatal Dose: Pure HCN: 50–60 mg; NaCN/KCN: 200–300 mg.
  • Fatal Period: 2–10 minutes for HCN gas; 30 minutes for oral cyanide salts.

Treatment

  1. Decontamination: Airway support, 100% oxygen, gastric lavage with 5% sodium thiosulfate.
  2. Specific Antidote (Eli Lilly Kit / Cyanide Antidote Kit):
    • Step 1: Amyl Nitrite inhalations (30 seconds every minute).
    • Step 2: Sodium Nitrite (3% solution, 10 mL IV over 10–20 min) — produces methemoglobin which binds cyanide to form cyanmethemoglobin.
    • Step 3: Sodium Thiosulfate (25% solution, 50 mL IV) — enables enzyme rhodanese to convert cyanide into non-toxic thiocyanate excreted in urine.
  3. Hydroxocobalamin (Cyanokit): 5 g IV infusion — binds cyanide to form non-toxic cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12). Preferred antidote.
Lee-Jones Test for Gastric Aspirate: To 5 mL gastric content add ferrous sulfate crystals + 5 drops of 2% NaOH. Boil, cool, and add 10 drops of 10% HCl. Greenish-blue color indicates cyanide.

Postmortem Findings

  • Strong odor of bitter almonds upon opening cranial cavity first.
  • Skin and postmortem patches show irregular bright pink / brick-red discoloration.
  • Glistening eyes with widely dilated pupils; early and marked rigor mortis.
  • Gastric mucosa eroded, blackened, and corroded due to alkaline cyanides (alkaline hematin formation).
  • Bright red fluid blood throughout body organs.
