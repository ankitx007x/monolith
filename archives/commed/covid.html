Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS)

Introduction

  • It is a communicable viral disease, caused by a new strain of coronavirus, which differs considerably in genetic structure from previously recognized coronaviruses.
  • Most common symptoms: Fever, malaise, chills, headache, myalgia, dizziness, cough, sore throat and running nose.
  • Chest X-ray findings: Typically begin with a small, unilateral patchy shadowing, and progress over 1–2 days to become bilateral and generalized, with interstitial/confluent infiltration.
  • Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) has been observed in a number of patients in the end stages.

Problem Statement on SARS-CoV-2

Status of SARS-CoV-2 in FY 2079/80 (Source: DoHS 2079/80):

  • SARS-CoV-2 positive: 187 cases with a positivity rate of 2.5%.
  • In 2021, SARS-CoV-2 surveillance was integrated into influenza surveillance. The National Integrated Influenza-SARS-CoV-2 Sentinel Surveillance Plan in Nepal was approved by MoHP on 9th Falgun 2078 (Feb 21, 2022). Implemented across all seven provinces, 15 sentinel hospitals are linked to this dual surveillance for Influenza and SARS-CoV-2.

Epidemiological Aspects

  • SARS was a relatively rare disease, with 8,273 cases as of 2003.
  • Healthcare workers, especially those involved in aerosol-generating procedures, accounted for 21% of all cases.
  • Maximum virus excretion from the respiratory tract occurs on about day 10 of illness and then declines.
  • Children are rarely affected by SARS.

Incubation Period

2 to 7 days.

Mode of Transmission

  • Direct or indirect contact of mucous membranes of eyes, nose, or mouth with respiratory droplets or fomites.
  • Use of aerosol-generating procedures in hospitals may amplify transmission.
  • The virus can survive for hours on common surfaces outside the human body, and up to four days in human waste.
  • Natural reservoir: Horseshoe bat.

Case Definition

An individual with laboratory confirmation of infection with SARS coronavirus who either fulfills the clinical case definition of SARS or has worked in a laboratory handling live SARS-CoV or storing clinical specimens infected with SARS-CoV.

Clinical Case Definition of SARS

A history of fever or documented fever AND one or more symptoms of lower respiratory tract illness (cough, difficulty in breathing, shortness of breath) AND radiographic evidence of lung infiltrates consistent with pneumonia or ARDS (or autopsy findings consistent with pathology of pneumonia or ARDS without an identifiable cause) AND no alternative diagnosis fully explaining the disease.

Diagnostic Tests for Laboratory Confirmation

a. Conventional RT-PCR and Real-Time RT-PCR:

  • At least 2 different clinical specimens (e.g., nasopharyngeal and stool specimens) OR
  • The same clinical specimen collected on 2 or more occasions during the course of the illness OR
  • A new extract from the original clinical sample tested positive by 2 different assays or repeat RT-PCR on each occasion of testing OR
  • Virus culture from any clinical specimen.

b. Serology (ELISA and IFA):

  • Negative antibody test on serum collected during acute phase, followed by positive antibody test on convalescent-phase serum, tested simultaneously OR
  • A four-fold or greater rise in antibody titre against SARS-CoV between acute and convalescent-phase serum specimens, tested simultaneously.

Complications

  • Viral pneumonia, pulmonary decompensation
  • Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)
  • Infection with nosocomial pathogens
  • Tension pneumothorax
  • Non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema

Treatment

  • Severe cases require intensive support.
  • Drugs used include Ribavirin (400–600 mg/d and 4 g/d), Lopinavir/ritonavir (400 mg / 100 mg), Interferon type 1, Intravenous immunoglobulin, and systemic corticosteroids.
  • Treatment efficacy of these therapeutic agents remains inconclusive.
  • There is no vaccine against classic SARS.

Prevention

  • Prompt identification of persons with SARS, their movements and contacts
  • Effective isolation of SARS patients in hospitals
  • Appropriate protection of medical staff treating these patients
  • Comprehensive identification and isolation of suspected SARS cases
  • Simple hygienic measures such as hand-washing after touching patients, use of appropriate and well-fitted masks, and infection control measures
  • Exit screening of international travelers
  • Timely and accurate reporting and sharing of information with authorities

Coronavirus Disease (COVID-19)

Past Questions: Write short note on: Preventive measures of COVID-19 Pandemic [4 Marks, 2080]

Introduction

Coronaviruses are a large group of viruses which may cause illness in humans and animals. Infections range from SARS, MERS, to COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2).

Problem Statement on COVID-19 (DoHS 2079/80)

  • 5,789 cases of COVID-19 were reported by EWARS in FY 2079/80. COVID-19 was partially reported from sentinel sites.
  • The COVID-19 pandemic 2076/77–2077/78 (2020–2021) resulted in fewer reported routine cases, but the 2079 (2022) outbreak was larger, with 56,338 cases and 88 deaths, marking it as the largest outbreak in Nepal to date.
  • In FY 2079/80, the COVID-19 vaccination program continued throughout the country:
    • 5–11 years age group vaccination was initiated.
    • Booster dose to 12–17 years age group was initiated.
    • Provision of 2nd booster dose.
  • In FY 2079/80, coverage of first and second dose of COVID-19 vaccination among total population was 83.1% and 83.6%. Provincial differences were observed with least coverage in Koshi and Karnali, and highest coverage in Bagmati and Gandaki provinces.

Epidemiological Factors

  • Agent: SARS-CoV-2
  • Host: Humans (Intermediate host: Not definitively known)
  • Environment: Crowded places. No distinct seasonal pattern has been identified.

Transmission

  • Direct and Indirect Contact: Through secretions such as saliva and respiratory droplets when an infected person coughs, sneezes, talks or sings. Droplet transmission occurs when a person is within 1 meter range. Virus enters through mouth, nose, eyes.
  • Airborne Transmission: Rare; possible during aerosol-generating procedures or in poorly ventilated indoor settings with infectious dose.
  • Fomite Transmission: Contaminated surfaces (ranges from a few hours to days depending on material, temperature, and humidity) followed by touching mouth, nose, or eyes.
  • Infectivity Period: Humans infected with SARS-CoV-2 can infect others 1–3 days before symptom onset, with highest viral load on day of onset and gradual decline over time. PCR positivity lasts 1–2 weeks for asymptomatic persons, up to 3 weeks for symptomatic cases, and longer in severe cases. Humans can also transmit the virus to other mammals (dogs, cats, farmed mink).
  • Other Modes: Urine and feces (virus found, but no clear transmission documented); plasma/serum (unclear); vertical transmission (no strong evidence).

Diagnosis

  • Clinical features with history of exposure
  • RT-PCR (Nasopharyngeal swab, Oropharyngeal swab, Bronchoalveolar lavage)
  • Chest CT scan and Chest X-ray (CXR)
  • Rapid Diagnostic Kits & GeneXpert PCR-based testing

Treatment & Management

  • No definitive curative antiviral treatment; treat symptomatically.
  • High-flow oxygen therapy & Mechanical ventilation for severe cases.
  • Remdesivir, Convalescent Plasma, and Dexamethasone.
  • Management Pillars: Testing, Isolation of cases, Tracing of contacts, Quarantine of contacts, Treatment, and Surveillance.

Prevention

Systemic & Healthcare Level Prevention

  • Identify suspect cases: Test and isolate the infected.
  • Identify close contacts: Quarantine and test if symptomatic.
  • SMS: Social distancing, Mask usage, Sanitization/Hand hygiene.
  • Contact and droplet precautions by Healthcare Workers (HCWs).
  • Airborne precautions by HCWs when performing aerosol-generating procedures.
  • Continuous use of surgical masks by HCWs and caregivers in all clinical areas.
  • Frequent hand hygiene, physical distancing, and respiratory etiquette.
  • Avoid crowded places and close-contact settings in confined spaces with poor ventilation.
  • Dedicated Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) led by Nursing Officers.
  • Prevent burnout and ensure stress management among healthcare personnel.

Individual Level Prevention

  • Maintain physical distancing (at least 1 meter), even if individuals do not appear infected.
  • Wear a mask in public, especially indoors or when physical distancing is not possible.
  • Choose open, well-ventilated spaces over closed ones; open windows indoors.
  • Clean hands often with soap and water or an alcohol-based hand rub.
  • Get vaccinated and follow local vaccination guidelines.
  • Cover nose and mouth with a bent elbow or tissue when coughing or sneezing.
  • Stay home if unwell.
  • Personal commitment: "If I get infected, no one else gets infected." Limit mobility—don't provide free transport to the virus.

Immunization (Vaccines Used)

  • Sinopharm (Beijing) BBIBP-CorV (Vero Cells)
  • Serum Institute of India Covishield (Oxford/AstraZeneca formulation)
  • Janssen (Johnson & Johnson) Ad26.COV2.S
  • Pfizer/BioNTech BNT162b2

Nepal Preparedness

  • Nepal Preparedness and Response Plan drafted.
  • Designation of dedicated COVID hospitals across provinces.
  • Organizational level preparedness: Testing, tracking, treatment, training, and isolation.
