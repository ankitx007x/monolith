Chicken Pox (Varicella)

Fast Track Clinical Science MBBS - 3rd Year | Communicable Diseases

Past Questions: Write short note on chicken pox. [2064/65]

Introduction

It is an acute, highly infectious disease caused by Varicella zoster (V-Z) virus characterized by vesicular rash that may be accompanied by fever and malaise.

Problem Statement

  • Global: Annual incidence of 4.2 million hospitalizations and 4,200 deaths.
  • National (Nepal): Frequent outbreaks including massive outbreaks reported in 2015 from Nawalparasi district and a recent outbreak in 2019 in Lalbandhi district, Sarlahi.

Epidemiological Determinants

Agent Factors

  • Agent: Human alpha herpes virus 3 (ds DNA, enveloped).
  • Primary Infection: Causes chickenpox.
  • Source of Infection: Case of chickenpox through oropharyngeal secretions and lesions of skin and mucosa.
  • Infectivity: 1 to 2 days before and 4 to 5 days after rash (crust is not infective).
  • Secondary Attack Rate: Up to 90% in household contacts as it is highly contagious.

Host Factors

  • Age: Children < 10 years (severe in adults). 50% cases in 5–9 years children. At least 90% of the population has had chickenpox by age 15, and 95% by young adulthood. 5–10% of population are susceptible to the disease in adulthood.
  • Immunity: Primary attack gives lifelong immunity (second attack is rare).
  • Pregnancy: Risk for fetus leading to congenital varicella syndrome, occurring in 0.4–2.0% of children born to mothers infected with VZV during the first 20 weeks of gestation.

Environmental Factors

  • In the coolest and driest months in tropics.
  • Periodic large outbreaks occur with an inter-epidemic cycle of 2–5 years.
  • Crowded areas favor transmission.
  • Heat-labile virus, so outside the host cell, VZV survives in the external environment for only a few hours, occasionally for a day or two, and is readily inactivated by lipid solvents, detergents, and proteases.

Transmission of Virus

  • VZV is highly transmissible via droplet infection and droplet nuclei.
  • Direct contact with characteristic skin lesions of the infected person, face-to-face contact.
  • Portal of entry: Upper respiratory tract or conjunctiva.
  • VZV can cross the placental barrier, causing congenital varicella.

Incubation Period

14–17 days (range: 10–21 days).

Clinical Features

1. Prodrome

Fever, malaise, headache, anorexia.

2. Exanthema

  • Rash occurs within 24–48 hours.
  • Centripetal spread: Macules appear in trunk and spread to face and extremities.
  • Evolution: Papules → Vesicles → Pustules → Crusts.
  • Appears in crops.
  • Pleomorphic rash: Pathognomonic for varicella (all stages of rash present simultaneously).
  • Rash fades with hypo/hyperpigmented lesion.
  • Scarring is unusual unless infected.
  • Pruritus present.
  • Mucosa involved.

Complications

  • Secondary skin infection
  • Pneumonia
  • Meningoencephalitis
  • Acute cerebellar ataxia
  • Transverse myelitis
  • Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)

Diagnosis

  • Clinical evaluation
  • PCR
  • Anti-IgM antibody
  • Tzanck smear: Presence of multinucleated giant cells.

Treatment & Management

  • Symptomatic: Antipyretic, Antipruritic, Good hygiene.
  • Isolation of patient.
  • Acyclovir / Valacyclovir / Famciclovir for 5 days started within 24 hours.
  • Varicella Zoster Immunoglobulin (VZIG).

Control & Prevention

Control Measures

  • Notifications: Isolation of cases for about 6 days after onset of rash.
  • Disinfection of articles soiled by nose and throat discharge.
  • Antiviral therapy (Acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir) for 5 days started within 24 hours.

Prevention

1. Varicella Zoster Immunoglobulin (V-ZIG)

To be given within 96 hr (preferably 72 hours) IM with a repeat dose in 3 weeks in conditions like:

  • Exposure to person with chicken pox / zoster
  • Immune compromised without history of infection/immunization
  • Premature infant in hospital
  • Pregnant women

2. Vaccination

A live attenuated varicella virus vaccine is safe and currently recommended for children between 12–18 months of age who have had chickenpox.

  • All children more than 1 year of age (upto 12 years) with no infection: single dose.
  • Seronegative adults more than 12 years of age: two doses given 4 to 8 weeks apart.
  • Contraindicated: Immunocompromised and pregnant women (therefore given varicella zoster immunoglobulin).
